# Sudan: Drone strikes killed at least 306 people in three weeks
23 October 2026 [Peace and Security](https://news.un.org/en/news/topic/peace-and-security)
UN human rights chief Volker Türk on Friday strongly condemned an escalation in drone strikes across Sudan this month, which killed at least 306 people and injured hundreds more in the first three weeks of October alone.
The strikes, attributed to both the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), hit homes, markets, hospitals, displacement sites, power stations and water sources in Kordofan, Darfur, Blue Nile, Khartoum and other states, according to the UN human rights office ( [OHCHR](http://www.ohchr.org/EN/pages/home.aspx) ).
“ **The increasing use of drones, already accounting for the majority of conflict-related civilian deaths in Sudan this year, is deeply disturbing and is exacting a horrifying toll** ,” Mr. Türk said.

## **Targeted by both sides**
In a particularly disturbing trend, the office documented numerous strikes on displacement sites, where people who had already fled the fighting had sought shelter.
Tweet URL
> [](https://twitter.com/UNHumanRights/statuses/2113624486884901220)
Between 6 and 10 October, alleged drone strikes attributed to military government forces on shelters for displaced people in Ghubeish and El Nuhud in West Kordofan **killed at least 25 civilians** , including women and children.
On 9 October, an alleged RSF drone struck a crowd of displaced people engaged in artisanal gold mining near a displacement site in Abu Shendi, Blue Nile, **killing at least 31 people, most of them women and children** .
**On 18 October, at least 139 people were killed when a transportation truck was hit in a “double tap” repeat strike** in Dibab, West Kordofan, an area controlled by the RSF militia.

“Scores of civilians have been killed, including internally displaced persons, and vital infrastructure and services have been eviscerated, driving further displacement and deepening the already severe humanitarian crisis,” [said](https://media.un.org/unifeed/en/asset/d366/d3665187) OHCHR Spokesperson Seif Magango.

## Security Council demands accountability
In a statement released Friday, the [Security Council](https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/) condemned the Abyei attack “in the strongest terms”, warning that the death toll is expected to rise as UNISFA continues to verify casualties. They stressed that attacks on civilians could amount to war crimes and called for a full, transparent and prompt investigation, with those responsible held to account.
