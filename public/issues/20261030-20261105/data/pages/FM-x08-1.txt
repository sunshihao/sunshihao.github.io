## Letter to Shareholders

### _November 2, 2026_

### I.

### It would be an understatement to say that our results this past quarter were extraordinary.

### There has seldom been another enterprise in postwar America that has grown at the scale and speed of our own.

### Our company generated $2.3 billion in revenue in the third quarter of the year, representing an absolutely striking 96% growth rate over the same period the year before.

### We have been at this for more than two decades now, and none of us has ever seen a business at this scale growing at this pace and with this level of sustained ferocity. Indeed, our sequential growth in U.S. commercial of 29% would be an astonishing _annual_ growth rate of any public software company in America.

![Image 4: Total Revenue ($B)](https://www.palantir.com/assets/xrfr7uokpv1b/EAnqvGtrlHj4DmHlILwNx/a44875ad08470edafc7fc404100c5b44/Q3-2026-Total_Revenue-English.png?quality=70&width=1800)

### The underlying math of our financial results is simple, even jarring.

### We have now essentially doubled our entire business since the same quarter last year.

![Image 5: Quarterly Revenue Annual Growth Rate](https://www.palantir.com/assets/xrfr7uokpv1b/77u6cOrmAa3KBoVjqvUkD5/17ae16519ae5cfd4706ac499b3ee7f30/Q3-2026-Quarterly_Revenue-English.png?quality=70&width=1800)

### We have stated our case consistently, quarter after quarter. Such results as ours come as little surprise to those sensitive and curious enough to listen. But the most profound shifts in technology cannot be understood without reflection. Whoever has ears ought to hear.

### II.

### Every industry in the world—from banks to insurers to hospitals and even once-storied law firms—will either be remade or set briskly aside.

### The outcomes will increasingly be binary, with winners and losers, and nothing in between.

### The lonely middle will be little more than a barren wasteland filled with those who were once legacy players who failed to heed the word and paid the ultimate price. The crowded bottom will be occupied by those who long ago lost their chance to build on what might once have been an insight or an edge, now marching inexorably toward irrelevance, their fate sealed, the envelope unopened.

### Our business has been fundamentally transformed.

### For some time, our listeners have heard us point to U.S. commercial as the primary driver of our growth. Now, for the first time, revenue from U.S. commercial has surpassed revenue from our U.S. government business.

### We generated $984 million this past quarter in the U.S. commercial market—a 148% increase from the same period the year before.

### This is not mere growth.

### It is a rate of expansion that represents a degree of intensity and sustained momentum that we believe is unmatched in recent economic history.

![Image 6: US Commercial Revenue - Q3 2026](https://www.palantir.com/assets/xrfr7uokpv1b/736ymAtgUvytgugD2zxE0F/7fdf4e01205fb984aec1510fa58b3895/Q3-2026-US_Commercial_Revenue-English.png?quality=70&width=1800)

### The demand for our sovereign AI solutions that we began building long before the language models arrived has been absolutely ravenous and remains far from sated in the United States; indeed, its lifespan can be measured in quarters, not years. Our advantage has only expanded with this demand.

### A sales cycle that once had been more akin to a long, unproductive, and unsatisfying flirtation has been condensed. What may have taken months and years—a slow, coy, and theatrical dance towards a contract—can now take days and weeks. Consummation happens quickly.

### Some leaders of business and government institutions believed they could delay the future; many simply gambled that they would not be around to reckon with the consequences of their inaction. The revolution is happening so quickly these leaders have been stunned to find that they cannot get out of town.

### And yet a broad swath of leadership across American industry has awakened to the significance of this moment.

### The questions surrounding the adoption of large language models force the world and its leaders to confront both a promise and peril that is totally unlike anything that has come before. In the best case, these models on their own generate limited value. In the far more likely outcome, they become agents of chaos. Lacking a grounding in the means of interfacing with the labyrinth of datasets, people, business units, use cases, and critical safeguards scattered across the modern enterprise, the LLM agent is a virtually unlimited liability, possessing a nearly unbounded destructive potential and a blast radius that tends towards infinity.

### There is simply no means of effectively and reliably connecting the language models to large enterprises without the operating model and capabilities we have pioneered.

### III.

### There was a time not too long ago when many would ask us, with an inquiring and searching tone, whether our business, as compelling and consequential as it seemed, would ever turn a profit.

### The subtext was that our decision to invest for years in building a foundational architecture, while noble perhaps, represented an idiosyncratic diversion at best and foolish misadventure at worst.

### And yet the verdict has now come in.

### We generated another record quarterly profit—$1.13 billion—an amount that dwarfs what we delivered in revenue not long ago.

![Image 7: English GAAP Chart - 2](https://www.palantir.com/assets/xrfr7uokpv1b/1V8iWLQmPxgeESuHb447A1/42b5684bac34307aab00a15293bbb3b8/Q3-2026-GAAP_Net_Income-English_-_11.2.26.png?quality=70&width=1800)

### This moment, remarkable as it is for us, remains the first scene of a first act.

### It is our firm and unwavering intent to take the entire market, the whole thing.

### We think it is more likely than not that our raw concentration of creative and technical talent will continue to define the genre and craft the definitive story of software itself over the coming decades.

### And it is not merely because we have hired the best and brightest, although we have.

### Our outperformance is, at a more structural level, the result of our wild and unruly organizational culture, as well as a radical distaste for the conventional means of marshalling and cultivating human talent.

### We are, it seems, the number one founder factory in the world, ahead of Stanford, MIT, McKinsey, Google, Harvard, Bain, and BCG, among many others, according to one recent tally making the rounds.

### IV.

### There is indeed an alignment problem, and a grave one.

### It is just not the one that society has been sold.

### The real problem is not the challenge of aligning the aims and capabilities of our technological creations with our interests.

### It is rather seeking alignment between the corporations that are constructing those technologies and the interests of the customers who utilize them as well as the greater public interest itself.

### We have no interest in building software that leeches the value and alpha from our partners—migrating what they own and have built and have earned to our systems and our accounts.

### And yet what once disappointed the bankers now seems to enthral them. Venture learned, and now the markets are learning, that the only things that are valuable are the things that they do not understand.

### Our success comes from aligning with our customers’ fundamental interests—in doing right by them. We were founded more than two decades ago and have served our partners across the U.S. military and its allies with pride through five administrations.

### Our unwavering commitment is to the American public and to the institutions and the elected leaders that serve it.

### It is simply our firm belief that if one is inclined to ask someone to risk their life in the defense of our nation, we must always make sure they have what they need to do their jobs and come home alive.

### In this way, we see ourselves as the pioneers of a genuine and viable antiwar movement.

### We are all, in no uncertain terms, living through what John Lewis Gaddis, the professor of naval and military history at Yale, presciently described decades ago as the long peace.

### And we will join anyone who seeks in earnest to advance an even longer one.

### Sincerely,

![Image 8: Alex Karp Signature](https://www.palantir.com/assets/xrfr7uokpv1b/3AeTcrjSQ6br3aPLEkG2kF/64919c219c00a755bfba0d9eeb9bdf59/Alex_Karp_Signature.png?quality=70&width=1800)
