# Apple cuts Taiwan App Store commission to 26%

Standard rate falls from 30% starting Saturday

 Oct. 30, 2026 14:46

![App Store. (Reuters, alamy photo)](https://image.taiwannews.com.tw/2026%2F10%2F30%2Fa526b4df9a6a48e3b0f2a022c92a241f.jpg "App Store. (Reuters, alamy photo)")

App Store. (Reuters, alamy photo)

4301

TAIPEI (Taiwan News) — Apple will cut its standard App Store commission in Taiwan from 30% to 26% on Saturday following an investigation by the Fair Trade Commission, [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202610300104.aspx) reported Friday.

The commission said Friday that it had accepted Apple’s proposal after reviewing its App Store business practices. The investigation examined whether Apple’s restrictions on app developers violated competition law.

Apple [confirmed](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=w9mxlhek) Thursday that the lower rate would apply to paid apps and in-app purchases through Taiwan’s App Store on iPhones and iPads. Developers will receive the reduced rate automatically without having to sign updated terms by Oct. 31, per [Tai Sounds](https://www.taisounds.com/news/content/76/296931).

The change will allow developers to keep more revenue from app sales and purchases. For every NT$1,000 (US$32) in sales subject to the standard commission, Apple will collect NT$260 instead of NT$300, leaving developers with an additional NT$40.

The investigation focused on Apple’s restrictions on developers and the fees it charges for sales through its App Store. Apple proposed lowering its commission under Article 28 of the Fair Trade Act, which allows regulators to suspend investigations when companies agree to take corrective action.

The commission said it would monitor Apple to ensure the company meets its commitments. The regulator may reopen the investigation if Apple fails to comply, circumstances change significantly, or the decision was based on incomplete or misleading information, per [Economic Daily News](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5612/9846853?from=edn_newest_index).

The commission also said it could launch separate investigations into other potentially unfair business practices by Apple.
