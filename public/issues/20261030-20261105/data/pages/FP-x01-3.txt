# Democrats set to win majority of governorships for the first time in 16 years

Democrats flipped governorships in Iowa, Ohio, Georgia and Texas.

By  [Kevin Hardy](https://stateline.org/author/khardy/ "Posts by Kevin Hardy")  -

[![](https://stateline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Acton-photo-1536x1024.jpg)](https://stateline.org/2026/11/04/democrats-set-to-win-majority-of-governorships-for-the-first-time-in-16-years/) 

Dr. Amy Acton speaks at a campaign rally in Cleveland ahead of her successful campaign for Ohio governor. Acton will become the first Democrat elected to lead Ohio in 20 years during a midterm election cycle that saw huge Democratic gains in states. (Photo by Nick Evans, Ohio Capital Journal)

Democrats are poised to control the majority of governor’s offices for the first time in 16 years after the GOP racked up major losses across the country.

Democrats flipped governorships in Iowa, Ohio, Georgia and Texas and defended competitive races in Maine and Wisconsin, according to unofficial results from The Associated Press and Decision Desk HQ.

Midterms are always tough for the party that holds the White House, but high prices and growing disapproval of President Donald Trump delivered even stronger than expected results for Democrats Tuesday.

“It’s a blue wave that was bigger than anyone had expected, and it’s having consequences across the country, including here in Texas,” said Mark Jones, a political science professor at Rice University in Houston. “Overall, Donald Trump has single-handedly turned many red states blue — or at least purple.”

By late Tuesday, Democrats had won races for attorney general and lieutenant governor for the first time in decades and were well on their way to flipping the Texas House.

“One of the real questions will be what do Democrats do with this power, and will they be able to retain it,” he said. “This has effectively opened a door for Democrats across the country.”

Gubernatorial races [proved historic](https://stateline.org/2026/11/03/in-historic-night-voters-elect-first-black-woman-first-black-republican-as-governors/) for both Democrats and Republicans on Tuesday with the election of the nation’s first Black woman governor and the first Black Republican governor.

But the Democratic party made major gains coast to coast, particularly in reliably conservative states, that could reshape state policy for years to come.

In Iowa, the election of Democrat Rob Sand ends [more than a decade](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/2026/11/03/democrat-rob-sand-wins-election-to-become-iowa-governor-ap-projects/) of Republican control of the legislature and the governor’s office.

In Ohio, Democrat Dr. Amy Acton’s win marks the [first time in 20 years](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/11/03/democrat-amy-acton-wins-race-for-ohio-governor-the-associated-press-projects/) that Ohio voters have selected a Democrat to lead the Buckeye State.

Former Atlanta Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms will become Georgia’s first Democratic governor [since 2002.](https://georgiarecorder.com/2026/11/03/keisha-lance-bottoms-will-be-georgias-next-governor-in-historic-win/)

While governors were on ballots in 36 states, the nonpartisan Cook Political Report with Amy Walter only identified [a dozen races](https://www.cookpolitical.com/ratings/governor-race-ratings) for governor as competitive. Those included: Alaska, Georgia, Florida, Iowa, Kansas, Michigan, Nebraska, Nevada, Ohio, Oregon, Texas and Wisconsin.

Republicans have controlled a majority of state governments since 2010. Before Tuesday’s election, Republicans boasted a 26-24 gubernatorial advantage over Democrats. But the GOP’s state legislative advantage was even stronger, with Republicans controlling 28 state legislatures and Democrats controlling 18 with divided control of chambers in three states, according to the National Conference of State Legislatures.

By 12:30 a.m. Wednesday, Democrats had notched  major legislative wins. Democrats broke the [GOP supermajority in Florida](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/11/03/democrats-escape-their-superminority-status-in-the-florida-house/), flipped control of the Wisconsin state Senate and flipped the North Carolina state House.

In Pennsylvania, Democrats won the GOP-held state Senate, seizing  a trifecta – control of the governor’s office and both chambers of the legislature – in Harrisburg.

“Tonight’s earth-shattering victory is undeniable proof that voters are fed up with the MAGA agenda,” Heather Williams, president of the Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee, said in a news release.

In Arizona, Democrats declared victory in a yearslong effort to win control of the legislature, proclaiming that Republicans would be in the minority at the state Capitol [for the first time in 60 years.](https://azmirror.com/2026/11/03/the-trifecta-democrats-declare-victory-in-fight-for-control-of-arizona-legislature/)
