# NY Post, Economist called out for Jensen Huang coverage

Jensen Huang, founder and CEO of Nvidia, speaks in Stanford University's CS 153 course on April 30, 2026. // Photo Photo by Anderseidesvik via Wikimedia Commons

By Randall

November 11, 2026

The Asian American Journalists Association (AAJA) is condemning the choice of words used in coverage about Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. 

Huang recently apologized to his family for having an affair and now is being investigated by Nvidia for possibility exposing information about the company to a Chinese spy.

In separate articles, the two publications used puns that AAJA fears “come at the expense of perpetuating racial stereotypes or casting entire communities under suspicion.”

According to AAJA, the Economist November 9th article used the tag line “Two Huangs don’t make a right.” Huang reportedly had an affair with Alice Huang (no relation). China has denied she is a spy, but U.S. intelligence agencies disagree.

“While wordplay may seem harmless, turning a common Asian surname into a punchline in a story about espionage and national security risks reinforcing harmful associations between identity and suspicion, wrongdoing or divided loyalties. The punchline reinforces stereotypes that real people have to live with,” AAJA said in its statement.

AAJA also cautioned against mixing Asian Americans and Chinese nationals as one of the same pointing out that “these are not interchangeable, and a person’s background or national origin should never be used to presume loyalties.”

AAJA hopes to meet with the leadership at both The Economist and the New York Post to discuss its concerns. It’s also urging the public to help flag similar coverage by notifying its media watch committee at [email protected].
