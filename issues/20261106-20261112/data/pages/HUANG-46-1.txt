# Who will decide Jensen Huang’s fate? Meet Nvidia’s board

Jensen Huang has long been almost synonymous with Nvidia. Now, suspicions of espionage have put the question of whether he will stay or go in the spotlight. 

Who will decide the fate of Huang and Nvidia? That may well lie in the hands of Nvidia’s 10 other directors. The board has said it will appoint a special committee of current directors to investigate the allegations surrounding Huang, with assistance from external legal advisers. 

These directors have diverse backgrounds, ranging from early investors who have been with Nvidia as it grew to finance and technology executives, and a scientist who specialises in how marine organisms swim. 

Lianhe Zaobao takes a look at the people who could shape Nvidia’s future. 

## 1) Nvidia’s board has no chair 

Nvidia’s board has, until now, kept a low profile. 

Unlike many listed companies, Nvidia has no board chair. Instead, it has an independent lead director. 

This board structure is highly unusual among large US companies. A report published by law firm Fenwick in 2025 found that only one company in the S&P 100 index had no chair: Nvidia. 

The same report, however, showed that as many as 60.6% of companies had the same person serving as both board chair and chief executive. 

According to Nvidia’s 2026 proxy statement, this structure is intended to “ensure that every director has an equal voice in Nvidia’s affairs and management decisions. The independent directors reassess the independent lead director role and its appointment annually.” 

The board believes its current leadership structure is appropriate. 

Apart from Huang, all 10 of Nvidia’s directors are independent. 

## 2) Who is the independent lead director? 

Nvidia’s current independent lead director, Stephen Neal, 77, joined its board in 2019. He also chairs the nominating and corporate governance committee. 

 

Neal is chairman emeritus and senior counsel at prominent US law firm Cooley, where he served as chief executive from 2001 to 2008.（Cooley website）

Neal previously chaired the board of denim clothing maker Levi Strauss & Co and has been involved in disputes and investigations concerning companies including General Motors. He also represented Walter Hewlett, son of HP co-founder William Hewlett, in an attempt to block HP’s merger with Compaq.
