# Alice Huang barred from re-entering Singapore after providing inaccurate information to police: MHA

Police investigations into Megaspeed and six other individuals are still ongoing, MHA said.

SINGAPORE – The Singapore authorities found insufficient evidence that former Megaspeed director Alice Huang had made inaccurate declarations regarding server purchases and other related offences, citing limited information received to date from the United States and other jurisdictions.

She was, however, issued a stern warning and barred from re-entering Singapore after police assessed that she had provided inaccurate information to downplay her role in Megaspeed, said the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) in a statement on Nov 12.

Huang, alleged to be a Chinese spy involved in an affair with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, had been investigated by Singapore police and customs as part of a larger probe into several companies, including Singapore-based cloud computing firm Megaspeed, and their key officers. The probe, which started in February 2025, was not based on any request from any country or sovereign entity, MHA said.

MHA said the police and Attorney-General’s Chambers (AGC) had requested help from the US authorities during a meeting on July 7, 2025, to get US-based server supplier Supermicro to cooperate with investigations.

But Singapore has “received limited information or assistance” on Supermicro despite repeated requests to the US Department of Justice and the Federal Bureau of Investigation since September 2025, the ministry added.

MHA said Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, was investigated for suspected involvement in possible offences in Singapore given her former position as Megaspeed’s CEO and chairperson.

The Hong Kong resident was not based in Singapore, and was not under investigation for espionage, it added.

Following consultations with the AGC, the police concluded in February 2026 that there was insufficient evidence to determine that Huang had committed any offences relating to the possible inaccurate declarations for which Megaspeed was being investigated, MHA said.

The police returned Huang’s passport on Feb 26, 2026, and she left Singapore on the same day, said MHA.

Other cases in the past have been dealt with in a similar way, MHA noted.

It added that police investigations into Megaspeed and six other individuals are still ongoing, and none of the six individuals has been in Singapore since investigations began.

MHA stressed that Singapore is committed to upholding its reputation as a trusted and reliable business hub.
