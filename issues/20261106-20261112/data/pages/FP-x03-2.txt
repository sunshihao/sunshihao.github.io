# House GOP sinks Hegseth impeachment

The move is a win for the Defense secretary, who said attempts to oust him amounted to an attack on American troops and the president. 

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) departs the U.S. Capitol after his articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth failed to pass a procedural House vote on Nov. 10, 2026. | Francis Chung/POLITICO

By Connor O'Brien, Mark Satter and Leo Shane III

11/10/2026 5:12 PM CST | Updated: 11/10/2026 6:02 PM CST 

The House on Tuesday defeated a Republican-led attempt to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, sparing him from a potentially damaging trial and handing President Donald Trump a major win.

The 211-204 procedural vote killed the effort by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) and ensured it would not move any further this Congress. Massie — who was the only Republican voting to keep the effort alive — had accused the Pentagon chief of eight charges that he alleged amounted to high crimes and misdemeanors.

“Republicans admit why they lost the election, but they’re paralyzed and can’t do anything to correct for it,” Massie said after the vote. “And I think that’s kind of a theme up here is that people know what the right thing is. They just can’t bring themselves to it because there’s some other political reality.”

The vote is a victory for Hegseth and the White House, which rallied behind the Pentagon chief as Massie and Democrats joined forces to remove him. During a rare Pentagon press conference Tuesday morning, Hegseth said the effort to oust him amounted to an attack on American troops and Trump.

Trump, in a Truth Social post following the vote, celebrated Democrats’ inability to advance the effort.

“The Democrats’ attempt to impeach Secretary of War Pete Hegseth has miserably FAILED! Every single Republican, except Thomas Massie, a major LOSER, voted in favor of Pete,” Trump posted. “Again, Victories in Venezuela and Iran, the Best Recruitment Numbers in the History of the Military (Long waiting lists to get in!), and Spirit like rarely seen before, led to the EXONERATION of a Great American Patriot.”

Speaker Mike Johnson kept the GOP ranks united, despite a midterm drubbing due in part to the unpopular Iran war that Hegseth oversaw. Johnson ripped the move as a political ploy.

“Thomas Massie is a disgruntled congressman, and the impeachment effort is a political gambit for attention,” Johnson told reporters ahead of the vote.

Backers of the impeachment fell short in part because 11 Democrats missed the vote. Just eight Republicans skipped it.

Rep. Eugene Vindman (D-Va.) said that some Democrats “were at an event that didn’t make it back in time, so it’s unfortunate. Really, should have never happened, but that’s the way it played out.”
