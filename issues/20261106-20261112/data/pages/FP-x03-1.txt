WASHINGTON (AP) — The House on Tuesday turned aside a resolution to impeach [Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth,](https://apnews.com/hub/pete-hegseth) halting an effort to remove the Pentagon chief for his conduct in office and his handling of [the war in Iran.](https://apnews.com/hub/iran)

The vote came as Republicans grapple with the fallout from [sweeping midterm losses](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-elections-congress-new-lawmakers-231d18d536141d9c19ac7e8cfca66eec) that some in the party have blamed on the war in Iran and the resulting surge in gas prices. But after President Donald Trump signaled strong support for Hegseth, GOP lawmakers largely stood by the secretary Tuesday, blocking the effort in a near party-line vote.

The effort was led by Kentucky Republican Rep. Thomas Massie and backed by Democrats. The vote Tuesday may only delay an impeachment push against Hegseth, with [Democrats set to take control](https://apnews.com/article/democrats-house-majority-iran-war-healthcare-tariffs-0658bbfc51d2bc23331714377cddb0d2) of the House and Senate in January.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to become the next speaker when Democrats take charge in January, stood firmly behind the push, saying Hegseth has “abused his power” and “undermined America’s national security.”

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer endorsed Hegseth’s impeachment as well.

Hegseth rebuked the effort Tuesday in his first news conference in months, portraying Massie as a rogue lawmaker and arguing he had committed none of the high crimes required in the Constitution for his removal. He framed the effort as an indictment against the nation’s service members and their successes and said it was undermining American forces “in real time.”

“This is not patriotism,” Hegseth said. “This is sabotage.”

Massie’s resolution included eight counts of impeachment. It largely focused on the war in Iran but also included references to Hegseth’s role in carrying out military actions in Yemen and Venezuela.

Massie, a Kentucky Republican leaving Congress in January after losing his primary to a Trump-backed opponent, says Hegseth has “knowingly executed and implemented unlawful orders.”
