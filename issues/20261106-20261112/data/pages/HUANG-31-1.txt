* Share:

OpenAI has privately told congressional offices that it supports three bills aimed at making advanced semiconductors and the tools used to make them harder for China to access, according to two people familiar with the matter granted anonymity to discuss private details.

The voice of support for the trio of measures, first reported by Punchbowl, is a pivot for the AI giant, which [toned down its previous advocacy for tightening export controls](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/20/openai-ai-chips-china-nvidia-01127385) in talks on Capitol Hill this year, a shift that came as the company deepened its financial ties with the U.S. chip giant Nvidia.

A third source familiar with the company’s strategy said they have been engaging with offices on the bills over the past several months.

Nvidia and its CEO, Jensen Huang, have been among the most vocal opponents of policies that would make it harder for the company to sell its advanced AI chips to China or other foreign countries. In particular, it has [lobbied on the legislation](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/29/nvidia-amd-trump-chips-china-ai-01096445) — the Chip Security Act and AI Overwatch Act — that lawmakers are now looking to attach to the [annual defense authorization bill](https://legislation.politicopro.com/bill/US_119_S_4784?q=national+defense+authorization) they aim to pass by year-end. The woman, an executive at a Singapore-based data center firm, allegedly helped funnel Nvidia chips to China in violation of U.S. export policies.

“Nvidia’s scandal, I think, has probably loosened them up a bit,” said one of the people.

OpenAI did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The company could benefit from more stringent controls of foreign sales of Nvidia’s chips, which are used in the continued build-out of its data centers and product development. Nvidia’s supply has not been able to keep up with the explosion in demand, and U.S. artificial intelligence labs have been struggling with shortages of the chips. China hawks argue the controls could also slow development by rival AI labs in China, giving the U.S. a legs up amid a tech race between Washington and Beijing.

The three measures would crack down on chip smuggling by requiring location-verification mechanisms; tighten restrictions on chipmaking equipment while pushing foreign countries to match U.S. curbs; and give Congress a chance to review licenses for advanced AI chip sales.

In the wake of Nvidia’s scandal, lawmakers on both sides of the aisle say it is increasingly likely Congress will include at least some of the measures sharpening export restrictions in the final defense bill.
