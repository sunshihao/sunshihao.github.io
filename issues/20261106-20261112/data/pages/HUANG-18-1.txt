## Beijing's Foreign Ministry rejected reports of an intelligence operation as Nvidia launches an independent investigation

China on Monday rejected allegations of a spying operation targeting Nvidia CEO [Jensen Huang](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-apologizes-firm-probes-affair-alleged-spy), dismissing reports of a suspected Chinese intelligence plot as a smear campaign against Beijing.

"The so-called Chinese spy narrative is purely a groundless accusation, slander, and a smear," Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun told reporters during a regular news briefing in Beijing on Monday.

The denial comes after Nvidia announced an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been the "target of a foreign intelligence effort."

The New York Times previously reported that U.S. intelligence officials determined Huang had developed a close relationship with Alice Huang, an unrelated Chinese business executive at Singapore-based company Megaspeed whom U.S. authorities suspect of [gathering information for Beijing](https://foxnews.com/politics/us-attorney-warns-chinese-espionage-extremely-pervasive-beijing-playing-long-game).

Huang acknowledged an extramarital affair in a letter to Nvidia employees Sunday but did not identify the woman involved. He pledged to cooperate with the company's investigation and emphasized his commitment to [U.S. national security](https://foxnews.com/tech/unvetted-foreign-workers-ai-firms-raise-national-security-alarms-advocates-warn).

Beijing has also pushed back against the broader reporting, with some Chinese state media suggesting the allegations were intended to undermine [U.S.-China relations](https://foxnews.com/politics/real-winner-xi-summit-revealed-trump-looks-usher-new-era-china).
