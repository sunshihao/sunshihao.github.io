Nvidia's Add To Watchlist board is meeting to decide whether to oust CEO Jensen Huang, CNBC confirmed late Wednesday, following reports of two extramarital affairs — one with an employee and another with an alleged Chinese spy. Shares of Nvidia fell more than 9% in post-market trading after The Wall Street Journal first reported that the board was preparing to force out Huang. That news landed minutes after The New York Times reported that Huang had a romantic relationship with Nvidia's vice president of health care.

The alleged relationship, according to the Times story, was with Kimberly Powell, vice president of health care at Nvidia. Powell's bio on the Nvidia website says she's "responsible for the company's worldwide healthcare business, including hardware and software platforms for accelerated computing, AI, and visualization that power the ecosystem of medical imaging, life sciences, drug discovery, and healthcare analytics."

Huang and Powell were reportedly captured on camera being affectionate with one another at a hotel in San Jose, California in March, according to the Times. 

Nvidia and Powell didn't respond to CNBC's request for comment. Heller, the crisis PR firm Huang reportedly hired as early as last year, did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

The report comes days after Huang admitted to an affair with a woman who is believed by U.S. intelligence of being a Chinese spy. The CEO was romantically involved with Alice Huang, according to The Financial Times, who U.S. intelligence officials believe is a spy for China's Ministry of State Security, The New York Times reported.

Nvidia stock chart.

Nvidia's board of directors has opened an independent investigation and hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to look into the spying allegations. 

"I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did," Huang wrote in the message to Nvidia staff on Sunday. "I am deeply sorry."

Most of Huang's Nvidia shares are co-owned with his wife, Lori Huang, and are held in trusts. About 800 million shares are held by those trusts. Huang holds about 70 million shares on his own and has limited economic control over the company.
