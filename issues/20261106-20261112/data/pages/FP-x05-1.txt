## **John Thune is reelected** as Senate GOP leader after midterm losses

The South Dakotan fought back efforts from some colleagues to delay leadership elections.

Senate Republicans reelected John Thune Tuesday to lead their party as it is thrust out of power in the next Congress.

The Senate GOP leadership team was reelected by acclamation inside a closed-door conference meeting that came after Republicans quashed the hopes of some conservatives to delay the leadership election.

Sen. Ron Johnson (R-Wis.) said Thune gave a “heartfelt speech” to colleagues Tuesday — with even Johnson, who was among those seeking a delay, saying the South Dakotan had been “very unfairly attacked” by the party’s base.

“This has never been anything against John Thune,” he said.

Thune said in a statement after the vote that Republicans “remain united in our commitment to advancing conservative priorities, working with President Trump and our colleagues in the House, and holding the line against Democrats’ efforts to enact their far-left, liberal agenda.”

“I appreciated the opportunity to have an open, honest, and transparent conversation with my colleagues today, and I am honored to have their continued trust and support to lead the Senate Republican Conference in the 120th Congress,” he said.

The depth of the Senate GOP losses, however, had many senators pressing for answers on what might change inside their chamber. At least seven GOP-held seats are now in Democratic hands, with Republicans behind in two uncalled races.

“This was the worst set of losses since Watergate,” said Sen. Josh Hawley of Missouri, who was among the GOP senators who supported delaying the leadership elections. “I mean, it’s an incredible repudiation of the focus of this Congress.”

Members in Hawley’s camp complained this week that Republicans didn’t do enough to deliver over the past two years and that GOP senators needed more time to figure out their next steps. But Republicans overwhelmingly voted to proceed with the elections, 31-15, before choosing to keep Thune in charge.

But Thune, who remains well-liked by his GOP colleagues, was never viewed as being in jeopardy and did not face a challenger for the minority leader post. Some conservatives had hoped President Donald Trump would step in and call for Thune to go if Republicans lost the majority, but Trump instead said repeatedly that he was staying out of the leadership race.

Not even Sen. Rick Scott of Florida, who has previously run twice to lead the party in the Senate, made a bid this time around.

Thune has a challenging task ahead of him: He’ll have to manage the final two years of the Trump administration, where the president could try to keep his grip over congressional Republicans, while working to prevent further GOP losses in 2028, when the Senate map is more favorable to Democrats.

Thune also has a hefty to-do list to manage even before the majority flips in January — including avoiding a mid-December government shutdown, potentially having to deal with an impeachment of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and moving other must-pass legislation such the annual defense policy package and a farm bill.

“The job is not for the faint of heart, for sure,” Thune said Monday night. “But I feel like I’ve got something to offer and to contribute ... as we think about now having to chart a slightly new direction.”

## The incoming Senate Appropriations chair **calls for December government funding punt**

Washington Sen. Patty Murray wants a short-term patch to keep federal agencies funded beyond the Dec. 11 shutdown deadline, to give Democrats negotiating leverage when they retake the majority.

![Jennifer Scholtes](https://www.politico.com/dims4/default/resize/100/quality/90/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic.politico.com%2F70%2Fa2%2Fa0a14a014f249edbfdb2b5e8ee7c%2Fjen-scholtes-headshot.png)

[Jennifer Scholtes](https://www.politico.com/staff/jennifer-scholtes)

11/10/2026, 6:45pm ET

* Share:

Federal cash expires in one month, and the Senate Democrat poised to run funding negotiations next year wants a short-term patch into January — when her party takes control.

Looking ahead to the Dec. 11 government shutdown deadline, Washington Sen. Patty Murray said in an interview Tuesday that a stopgap is the best way to ensure Congress passes final, full-year spending bills that fulfill the demands of voters who overwhelmingly chose Democrats on Election Day.

“Voters handed us a huge mandate to write these bills that reflect their priorities,” said Murray, the incoming Senate Appropriations chair. “So how we can lower costs and end corruption and end the war in Iran — the best way for us to do that is to do a short-term CR and then write these funding bills for next year.”

Murray said she wants to boost nondefense dollars for priorities like childcare, education and infrastructure next year, while reining in defense spending and cracking down on President Donald Trump’s moves to shirk Congress’ funding power.

Democrats are still discussing how they might use the December government shutdown deadline to end the Iran conflict, she added — including whether the party will cut off cash for the military operation as a condition of voting to keep federal operations afloat.

“The devil will be in the details,” Murray said, adding that Democrats are “looking at all the options” for ending the war.

Democrats will also demand that any December funding bill extend language blocking the Trump administration from finalizing its plans to put political appointees in charge of approving federal grants, Murray said. Then, when her party takes control of both the House and Senate in January, the new majority will insert new provisions into funding bills designed to stop Trump from shifting, withholding and canceling federal spending Congress mandates.

That includes blocking the president from unilaterally canceling billions of dollars next fall, like he [did again this year](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/25/trump-declares-1b-canceled-in-pocket-rescission-in-another-affront-to-congress-funding-power-01093960), and holding oversight hearings to probe the administration’s spending tactics, Murray said: “I am going to make sure that we Trump-proof every one of our bills that we write next year, so this administration follows the letter of the law.”

Murray, who is currently the top Democrat on the Appropriations Committee, is expected to wield the gavel next year alongside Sen. Lisa Murkowski in the top Republican spot. The Alaska lawmaker is a longtime friend of Murray’s — and a partner over the last two years in [pushing back against Trump’s moves](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/09/lisa-murkowski-trump-appropriations-01155181) to claw back funding Congress already approved.

“She and I have a really good friendship,” Murray said of Murkowski. “We have a good, productive and strong working relationship. And I think she will represent her caucus well and be willing to work with us on any differences.”

## 2 key Democrats signal **tougher scrutiny of Nvidia** chip exports

Comments from the California lawmakers come as Congress weighs new limits on the sale of advanced semiconductors.

11/10/2026, 5:30pm ET

Two leading House Democrats are calling for additional scrutiny of Jensen Huang and the Trump administration after recent revelations that the top Nvidia executive was in a relationship with a Chinese spy.

In separate live interviews Tuesday afternoon at POLITICO Pub’s B20 USA, California Rep. Ted Lieu — in line to be chair of the House Democratic Caucus when his party retakes the majority in January — declared that the Trump administration has put national security at risk by being “1000%” too deferential to the AI-chipmaker.

And Rep. Zoe Lofgren, another California Democrat expected to chair the House Science Committee next year, said concerning reports about Huang’s lobbying activities could make Congress more likely to restrict U.S. sales of AI chips to China — though she stopped short of predicting new limits.

“The allegations are very serious,” said Lofgren.

Nvidia is among the chip manufacturers that have [lobbied the Trump administration](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/29/nvidia-amd-trump-chips-china-ai-01096445) to stop lawmakers from restricting exports to China, including through outreach to officials at the White House and at senior levels of the Commerce Department.

The export proposals have taken on added significance amid scrutiny of Huang’s ties to a woman whom U.S. officials believed was a Chinese intelligence asset, according to [reports by the New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/08/technology/nvidia-investigation-jensen-huang-china.html). Huang acknowledged in an email to Nvidia employees on Sunday that he had an extramarital affair, prompting the company’s board to launch an investigation.

Nvidia has insisted it complies with export laws and that its customer conversations do not involve sensitive trade secrets or national security information.

Lofgren said the recent reports could prompt Congress to tighten controls on chip exports: “Jensen didn’t lobby me, but I think there are a lot of reports that he did lobby the Trump administration, and certainly we need to make sure the national interest is protected.”

Democrats are also poised to compel numerous Trump officials and private tech sector executives to testify before several committees in the new Congress — and Nvidia is now likely to be at the very top of the list.

“Clearly there will be an investigation,” Lofgren said.

Lieu accused the administration of inexplicably reversing a “common sense policy” of forbidding chip sales to China, laying out a timeline for that policy decision he said appeared suspicious.

“When the Trump administration came in, they agreed with the Biden administration that selling advanced AI chips to China hurts U.S. national security … and then one day out of the blue, Donald Trump says, ‘I’m going to sell advanced AI chips to China,’” Lieu said.

He also intimated that Nvidia might have been about to make a “make a whopping donation” to the Trump’s White House ballroom project, and nodded to media reports suggesting that the president knew about Huang’s relationship with the spy but stopped investigating the company anyway.

## What the Congressional Black Caucus’ next leader **wants Democrats to focus on**

![Cheyanne M. Daniels](https://www.politico.com/dims4/default/resize/100/quality/90/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic.politico.com%2Fc3%2F39%2F10b1df684862a7d2a534ff0fe49a%2Fcheyanne-daniels.jpeg)

* Share:

The incoming leader of the Congressional Black Caucus urged his party to focus on affordability and environmental justice in the next Congress — and the voting rights fight that ripped the party apart the last time Democrats controlled both chambers.

In an interview Tuesday, Rep. Troy Carter (D-La.) said the CBC will look to tackle affordability, health care, environmental justice, economic parity and “of course” the John R. Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act in the new Congress.

“If you ask me to order them, I would tell you they’re all going to be coming at you simultaneously,” said Carter, who is poised to become the next chair of the caucus. The Lewis bill, he added, will be “the mechanism for which all other actions flow.”

The priorities aren’t much different from what Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries touted as [the party’s plans](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-elections-democrats-affordability-corruption-018019529b14a6b3bc4a343a4e17c44f) ahead of Election Day. But Democrats have looked to pass some version of voting rights legislation since the original blue wave during President Donald Trump’s first midterms, only to be stymied by Republican opposition and a brutal intraparty fight over the Senate filibuster.

Different iterations of a voting rights bill have been introduced by Democrats since 2019. While sometimes changing in size and scope, at its most expansive it would broadly [remake the American elections system](https://www.politico.com/news/2022/01/14/democrats-voting-rights-megabill-left-out-527095), from reinstating [a gutted provision of the Voting Rights Act](https://www.politico.com/newsletters/weekly-score/2021/03/29/where-and-what-is-the-john-r-lewis-voting-rights-act-794317) to banning partisan gerrymandering and creating public financing of congressional campaigns.

The [version introduced this Congress](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/14/text) focused specifically on the Voting Rights Act and restoring [“preclearance” requirements](https://www.politico.com/story/2013/06/supreme-court-voting-rights-act-ruling-093324) — that states or jurisdictions with a history of discrimination get preapproval from the federal government or courts before changing voting laws — and not even more wholesale changes to the country’s election systems.

When Democrats last controlled Congress following the 2020 election, [the sweeping bill passed the House](https://www.politico.com/news/2021/03/03/government-election-house-reforms-473545) amid stiff GOP opposition. It ultimately died in the Senate after then-Sens. Joe Manchin (D-W.Va.) and Kyrsten Sinema (D-Ariz.) [fought to keep the filibuster](https://www.politico.com/news/2021/06/22/elections-stepback-democrats-strategy-495578), effectively killing the bill, which did [not have enough GOP support](https://www.politico.com/news/2021/06/22/senate-elections-reform-bill-495487) to clear the 60-vote threshold.

Under Democratic control, voting rights legislation has recently gotten the symbolic designation as the first piece of legislation introduced in either chamber.
