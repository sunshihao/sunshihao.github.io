# Gold falls as hot inflation figures, higher dollar weigh

Published Tue, Nov 10 2026 10:49 PM EST Updated Wed, Nov 11 2026 4:27 PM EST

Gold prices edged higher on Wednesday after a nearly 3% slide in the previous session, but gains were limited as stronger-than-expected U.S. inflation data cemented expectations of a Federal Reserve interest rate hike next month, weighing on bullion.

Christopher Furlong | Getty Images

Gold prices extended the previous ‌session's fall on Wednesday as hotter-than-anticipated US inflation figures and hawkish comments from Federal Reserve policymakers boosted the dollar and raised the chances of a bigger interest rate hike next month.

Spot gold Add To Watchlist fell ⁠1.1% ‌to $3,984.97 an ounce after climbing as high as $4,066.77 earlier in the session. U.S. gold futures Add To Watchlist for December delivery fell nearly 1% to $3,991.4 per ounce.

"CPI came out a little hotter than expected, and the market is interpreting that it ‌warrants higher rates at the December meeting," said Bob Haberkorn, senior market strategist at StoneX, referring to the final Federal Reserve policy meeting of the year.

Gold fell about 3% to $4,013.29 on Tuesday after ​data showed U.S. consumer prices rose more than expected in October, with the CPI increasing 0.4%. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast a 0.3% rise.

Ahead of the CPI data, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a core ⁠inflation reading of 0.3% in October would prompt him to consider a half-point hike ‌at the December meeting.

While markets are fully pricing ‌in a 25-basis-point hike in December, bets on a 50-basis-point increase in December also rose to 13.2% from 0%, boosting the dollar to a four-week ⁠high.

A higher dollar makes greenback-priced bullion more expensive for overseas buyers, while ⁠non-yielding bullion also loses its appeal to interest-bearing assets in a high-interest-rate ⁠environment.

However, Haberkorn said, "there's been strong buying in the third quarter of gold by central banks. That's really kept the floor on ​gold despite the short-term fundamental news, ‌which is the Fed and interest rates working against it."

Among other metals, spot palladium gained 0.5% to $1,030.70 per ounce, having earlier hit its highest level since October 22.

Palladium markets may have reached the point where auto catalyst demand erosion intersects with recovering secondary supply, turning a ​decade of scarcity into a persistent ‌surplus, Deutsche Bank said in a note.

Spot silver rose 0.4% to $51.69 per ounce.
