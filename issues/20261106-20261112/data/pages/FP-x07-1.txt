# Supreme Court allows Trump’s Big Bend construction to continue 

The justices granted the Trump administration’s emergency appeal seeking to lift an order halting work in Big Bend National Park and surrounding areas. 

Protesters appear along the U.S. border in the Big Bend region, Sept. 26, 2026, in Study Butte, Texas. | John Locher/AP

By Josh Gerstein

11/09/2026 4:55 PM EST

The Supreme Court is allowing President Donald Trump to proceed with border wall construction in Texas’ remote Big Bend region, despite claims from environmentalists, Native Americans and ranchers that the project will harm the area’s residents and wildlife.

The justices ruled, 8-1, Monday in favor of the Trump administration’s emergency appeal seeking to lift a district judge’s order last month halting work in Big Bend National Park and surrounding areas. Administration officials have said the border wall won’t run through the park itself, but crews began work including road construction in park lands in August.

In an unsigned opinion, the high court said a 2005 law gives the secretary of Homeland Security the power to waive environmental and other legal requirements “in areas of high illegal entry into the United States.”

Congress’ passage of that statute “reflects a clear preference for expeditious border construction over the very harms” asserted in the lawsuit, the justices added.
