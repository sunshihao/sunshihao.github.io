## Sanders says he won’t support Schumer as majority leader

Schumer is facing pockets of opposition to lead his party, but no one has officially said they’ll run against him.

Calen Razor

11/10/2026, 4:22pm ET

Sen. Bernie Sanders said Tuesday he will not support Minority Leader Chuck Schumer as leader of the Democratic caucus next year.

Sanders announced his decision at a news conference with Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.) shortly after Democrats huddled in their first full caucus meeting since returning to Washington following sweeping midterm election victories.

Schumer this week has begun to face pockets of opposition within his ranks, and several outside advocacy groups have weighed in against his retaking the perch. But no other senator has officially entered the race against him.

“I personally have known Chuck Schumer for over 30 years, and I like Chuck. Nobody who works harder than Chuck Schumer,” Sanders (I-Vt.) said. “But in my view, the time is now for change, and I will not be supporting Chuck to be reelected as the Democratic leader.”

Slotkin added she is also a “no” on any candidate until they can articulate a plan outlining Democrats’ priorities in the first 100 days in the new Congress.

Schumer responded to Sanders’ announcement during a press conference Tuesday.

“I’m confident I will be elected majority leader. And our caucus is really united on bringing down costs, on ending the corruption, on ending the chaos, on ending the war. We have great unity in our caucus and I’ll be proud to lead it as majority leader,” the New York Democrat said.

Sanders said the two senators had heard out Schumer and had a serious discussion about his ideas going forward: “Was it as definitive as it should be? In my view it was not.”

Neither senator has identified who they might support instead, though both acknowledged that Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-Md.) is one name in consideration.

“We had a productive conversation,” Slotkin said of her Monday meeting with Van Hollen. “At the same time, I have the same feeling about anyone who runs as I do about Sen. Schumer. I want to see what that plan is. I want to see how it represents newer members. I will require that for anyone.”

Pennsylvania Democratic Rep. Madeleine Dean is “considering” a run for Senate in 2028 — including in a potential primary against incumbent Sen. John Fetterman.

“I’m going to be considering it,” Dean said in an interview Tuesday regarding a potential Senate run.
