The Nasdaq Composite fell on Thursday as investors assessed the departure of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and the outlook of the conflict in the Middle East.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq dropped 0.39% to end at 25,366.09, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 189.40 points, or 0.36%, to 52,297.09. The S&P 500 fell 0.35% and closed at 7,592.40.

Nvidia's sharp drop today is exposing one of the quirks of the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

Shares of the chip firm are down more than 5% today, after CEO Jensen Huang resigned amid a extra-marital affair scandal. The tech giant is dragging down the market-cap weighted S&P 500, where it makes up about 6% of the index. But Nvidia only represents about 2% of the Dow on a price-weighted basis, despite being one of the largest companies in the world by market value.

The picture would look very different if the Dow's 30 components were weighted by float-adjusted market cap rather than by price. According to CNBC calculations, Nvidia would make up more than 15% of the blue chip index under that methodology, and its decline today would subtract about 80 basis points from the Dow's return.

Other mega-cap stocks, like Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon offset part of that drag. Still, a market-cap weighted version of the Dow would be down about 0.5% today, compared to an actual Dow which is little-changed going into the close.

Cleveland Federal Reserve President Beth Hammack said Thursday she doesn't see the need for aggressive interest rate increases ahead.

While Hammack still supports rate hikes as the Fed struggles to get inflation back to its 2% target, she advocated for a gradual approach that the economy can adapt to more easily.

"I think we need to move rates higher to get to a restrictive stance of policy to bring inflation back down to 2%. My view is that if we go steadily in smaller increments, more frequent, smaller rises, it's easier for businesses and households to manage around that," she sad. "It's less disruptive to the overall economy than moving in sharper, more aggressive movements."

Earlier in the week, Fed Governor Christopher Waller raised the specter of a half-point hike at the December meeting if the inflation data comes in worse than anticipated. However, Hammack said she doesn't think that's necessary, at least for now.

"If we get behind the curve, and I don't think we're behind the curve today, but if we get behind the curve, then that might be something that we need to consider," she said. "So I'll certainly have an open mind walking into that December meeting."

Rolls-Royce reiterated its full-year profit and cash flow guidance Thursday as demand for power generation equipment used in data centers surged.

Order intake in its power systems business rose 62% in the first nine months of the year, as orders for gas engines nearly tripled year-on-year, driven by power demand from data centers, the company said in a trading update.

The unit's order backlog at the end of September stood at a record £8.6 billion ($11.4 billion).

The British group highlighted strong demand across its power systems, civil aerospace and defense businesses, as it continues to expect underlying operating profit of between £4.7 billion and £4.9 billion in 2026.

In defense, it highlighted progress on military aircraft programmes, including the MQ-25A autonomous refuelling aircraft and the Global Combat Air Programme.

It also said demand remains strong across both widebody and business aviation, as its large engine order backlog grew to around 2,500 engines.

"Given our strong progress this year, we will revisit our mid-term targets at our Full Year results in February," said CEO Tufan Erginbilgic.

It also announced an interim £1.5 billion share buyback as part of an existing program through 2028.
