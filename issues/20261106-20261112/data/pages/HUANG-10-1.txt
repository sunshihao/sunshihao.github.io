# Lawmakers want answers after Nvidia’s Huang steps down

Nvidia’s Jensen Huang resigned Thursday as CEO amid an explosive spy scandal, but Washington isn’t letting him off the hook.

Lawmakers on both sides of the aisle said they still want to hear from the ex-Nvidia chief amid national security concerns about his involvement with a woman who U.S. officials concluded was passing information to China.

“Jensen and I have been very at odds, as some of you already know, but Nvidia is a very important company to the United States of America,” House Foreign Affairs Committee Chair Brian Mast (R-Fla.) told reporters Thursday. “I don’t want to see anything hurt Nvidia.”

“But he has some very serious questions to answer,” he added.

Mast, who has long pushed for stricter chip export controls, said the former Nvidia CEO “agreed to come” in for a briefing that will likely be “under oath, transcribed, video recorded,” although negotiations are ongoing on the specifics.

Jensen Huang stepped down from his role leading the chipmaker on Thursday morning. It followed a bruising couple of days for the tech mogul and the company, which he founded in 1993 and turned into one of the most valuable firms in the world.

The former Nvidia chief admitted Sunday to having an affair with Alice Huang, a onetime executive at the Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed who is suspected of working as a Chinese intelligence asset.

The explosive revelations prompted bipartisan calls for an investigation into Jensen Huang and Nvidia, which don’t seem to be going away following his resignation.

Three top House GOP lawmakers — Mast; Rep. John Moolenaar (Mich.), chair of the House Select Committee on China; and Rep. Rick Crawford (Ark.), chair of the House Intelligence Committee — asked Jensen Huang last week to appear before Congress following initial reports of the affair.

Moolenaar said Thursday that his team is engaged in talks with Nvidia about having Jensen Huang testify. But the timing remains unclear, he added, not least because of the shake-up surrounding Jensen Huang’s resignation.

“I believe it’s important that we hear from him directly, as soon as possible,” the Michigan Republican said.

“I think it’s important that we have a deeper understanding of the national security concerns: How much information was gained by Chinese intelligence? The extent of their influence in terms of influencing policy discussions, and also chip diversion, either through smuggling or remote access,” Moolenaar added. “We want the entire picture.”

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) welcomed Jensen Huang’s resignation, but he said it’s not enough to allay concerns about the potential threat.

“It was the right thing to do,” he said Thursday during a press conference in the Capitol. “Now we have to get to the bottom of whether America’s national security has been compromised.”

Jeffries, who is poised to become Speaker in the new Congress come January, also amplified his earlier call for intelligence officials in the Trump administration to give a classified briefing to all members of Congress specifically on the Nvidia situation.

He said Democratic Reps. Jim Himes (Conn.), the incoming chair of the Intelligence Committee, and Gregory Meeks (N.Y.), who’s expected to lead the Foreign Affairs panel, will take the lead for House Democrats as they dig into the national security implications of the Jensen Huang scandals.

“We’ve seen the tip of the iceberg,” Meeks told reporters Thursday. “The board probably did what it had to do. I have other questions. This doesn’t make it go away.”

Sen. Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) similarly told Fox Business that Jensen Huang’s resignation “does not absolve Nvidia from doing a full investigation of what happened” and “what sort of sensitive information may have been transmitted.”

“We need to do our own investigations as well from the Department of Justice,” he added.

As they prepare to take control of both chambers of Congress, Democrats are also zeroing in on what the Trump administration knew and when.

President Trump was briefed about a year ago that the U.S. intelligence community believed Alice Huang was an operative for China’s government, The New York Times first reported last month. The president did not do anything with the information at the time, the Times reported, and even invited Jensen Huang aboard Air Force One when he flew to China in May.

But Trump said earlier this week he was told about the issue “not too long ago.”

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) said Thursday that Congress “must demand answers” from Jensen Huang and others at Nvidia, while also arguing the Trump administration should answer for what it did after learning of Jensen Huang’s relationship.

“Were government officials told not to investigate because of Trump’s relationship with Mr. Huang? What did Trump know when he personally invited Mr. Huang aboard Air Force One?” Schumer wrote in a post on the social platform X.

Other reports emerged this week that the Commerce Department last year paused its investigation into Megaspeed and whether it helped smuggle Nvidia’s advanced AI chips to China despite U.S. export controls. Reports said Commerce passed the probe off to the Department of Justice (DOJ), though neither agency confirmed this. The DOJ declined to comment.

Axios also reported Wednesday that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick stalled an authorization from Trump and other agency heads to crack down on firms suspected of diverting Nvidia’s chips to Congress.

“Secretary Howard Lutnick and Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler must explain why they have not closed export control loopholes and act today to ensure our advanced technology is not fueling China’s military,” Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.), a frequent critic of Jensen Huang, said Thursday.

Rep. Ted Lieu (D-Calif.), who is seeking to become House Democratic Caucus Chair next year, pointed to these reports, telling The Hill, “I wish the administration had disclosed that to Congress and the American people.”

Other Democrats underscored Congress’s investigative role, saying they don’t have confidence in the Trump administration to take the lead given the president’s ties to Jensen Huang.

“Normally, I would say the Department of Justice should take a look at what happened there, [but] I don’t know that they’re up to the task right now. Certainly there’s a lane for congressional oversight,” Rep. Glenn Ivey (D-Md.) said.

“We’ve got to get a better understanding of what happened there, and what the White House knew, and when they knew it, too,” Ivey added. “To the extent they knew some of that was going on but still let things move forward without disclosure, I think is disturbing.”
