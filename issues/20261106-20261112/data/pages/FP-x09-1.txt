WASHINGTON (AP) — [Sen. Bernie Sanders](https://apnews.com/hub/bernie-sanders) said Tuesday he would not be supporting [Sen. Chuck Schumer](https://apnews.com/hub/charles-schumer) as the Democratic majority leader in the new Congress, setting up a seismic confrontation after the party’s sweep to power in the [midterm elections](https://apnews.com/projects/elections-2026/).

Sanders was joined by Democratic Sen. [Elissa Slotkin](https://apnews.com/article/elissa-slotkin-democrats-iowa-trump-37aa9836f708f5f7650315df2cacca0f) of Michigan at a news conference at the Capitol, and together they said there needs to be a new generation of leadership and a vigorous debate about the party’s direction and future. A number of incoming Democratic senators have echoed the call, signaling that they would support another candidate over Schumer.

“The time is now for change,” said Sanders, the independent from Vermont and essentially the father of modern progressivism in the U.S. “And I will not be supporting Chuck to be reelected as the Democratic leader.”

Slotkin, who said she is not actively seeking the job herself, explained that she wants to see potential Senate leaders present a full plan about the priorities for the new majority.

“I’m a no until and unless I see that from anyone,” she said.

The showdown has been building as Democrats marched across the Senate map to flip Republican-held seats far beyond those that were initially competitive, including in Texas, Kansas and South Carolina.

Schumer recruited high-profile candidates that helped secure the majority, including Sen.-elect Sherrod Brown in Ohio and Sen.-elect Roy Cooper in North Carolina. But his preferred candidates also lost primaries in Michigan and Maine, with the progressive candidates prevailing and then going on to win their general elections.

Democrats, particularly in the party’s progressive wing, have been bombarding congressional offices with phone calls saying it’s time for new leadership. Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed and other progressives have pushed for Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland as a potential contender.
