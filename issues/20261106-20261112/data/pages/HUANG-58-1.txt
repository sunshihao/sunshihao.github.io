# Racist disruption shouted down by crowd at Seattle Diwali festival 

 Nov. 11, 2026 at 6:00 am Updated Nov. 11, 2026 at 6:00 am 

By 

 Ann Duan 

Seattle Times staff reporter

U.S. Rep. Pramila Jayapal and a group of colleagues are drafting a letter to the Department of Justice demanding two men be investigated for disrupting a Diwali celebration with racist signs Saturday afternoon at the Seattle Center Armory.

Dozens, including Democratic and Republican lawmakers, have condemned the disruption of the South Asian festival of lights, celebrated by Hindus, Jains, Sikhs and other religious communities. The holiday represents good defeating evil, knowledge defeating ignorance and light defeating darkness.

A group of high schoolers from an Eastside-based dance school, focused on the classical dance form of Kuchipudi, were performing onstage when two far-right content creators burst into the venue hoisting an American flag and signs bearing xenophobic language.

“Stop Hindu replacement of Christians,” one sign read. The other sign used an anti-Hindu slur.

Videos of the encounter showed the men yelling at the crowd as witnesses, including a handful of Seattle Kraken fans, rushed to push them away. 

## ‘Hateful, right-wing extremists’

One of the sign-waving men, Jake Lang, spent years behind bars after taking part in the Jan. 6, 2021, insurrection, according to federal court records. Jayapal said he never should have been released, but President Donald Trump pardoned him in 2025 — another decision Jayapal believed should never have been made.

“And now,” she said, “he travels across the country doing this as a traveling provocateur, drumming up these other hateful, right-wing extremists to go with him.”

Ideally, Jayapal said she’d like to see the two men be arrested “when they do these hate crimes across the country.” 

Thomas Allen, who accompanied Lang, had been charged with assault after injuring protesters demonstrating for transgender rights in September in Portland, according to The Oregonian newspaper.

Earlier this week, Jayapal decried racist rhetoric she said has fueled violence against Indian Americans. In a video posted to social media, she pointed to foiled plans for a mass shooting targeting an Illinois Hindu temple last month. The 19-year-old gunman’s weapon jammed outside the temple minutes before he planned to carry out an attack, investigators said.

In an interview, Jayapal said she hopes Democrats can change the narrative after seizing control of the House, Senate and statewide positions in the midterms.

“I think we’re going to have a lot of accountability hearings,” she said. “I know in the Judiciary Committee, we will take on anti-immigrant hate.” 

Jayapal added: “We will call on the Department of Justice to testify to us on what they are doing to counter white nationalist extremism and xenophobia and hate.”
