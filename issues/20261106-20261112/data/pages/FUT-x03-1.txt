ISSN: 1554-9089 World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates Office of the Chief Economist Agricultural Marketing Service Farm Service Agency Economic Research Service Foreign Agricultural Service WASDE - 677 Approved by the World Agricultural Outlook Board November 10, 2026 WHEAT: The outlook for 2026/27 U.S. wheat this month is for unchanged supplies, slightly lower domestic use, steady exports, and modestly higher ending stocks. Domestic use is reduced by 3 million bushels, all on lower food use. The NASS Flour Milling Products report indicated a 2 percent year-to-year decline in wheat ground for flour in the July through September quarter. Total wheat exports are unchanged at 750 million bushels, but there are offsetting by-class changes for White and Hard Red Winter (HRW). White wheat exports are raised on continued strong sales and shipments with White wheat priced competitively against other exporters to Asian destinations. Conversely, HRW exports are lowered on continued weak sales and shipments as HRW exports are now the second lowest on record. Projected all wheat ending stocks are raised by 3 million bushels to 743 million but are still 19 percent below last year. The season-average farm price is lowered $0.10 per bushel to $6.20 on NASS prices reported to date and expectations for futures and cash prices for the remainder of the marketing year. This month’s 2026/27 global wheat outlook is for higher supplies, greater consumption, modestly lower trade, and larger stocks. Supplies are projected up 6.4 million tons to 1,110.4 million, primarily on higher production for Argentina, Australia, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine. Argentina and Australia are both raised 1.5 million tons to 23.0 and 32.5 million, respectively, on continued widespread favorable conditions as both countries now move into their harvests. Global consumption is raised 1.7 million tons to 829.6 million, mostly on higher feed and residual use for Australia, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine more than offsetting lower food, seed, and industrial use for Turkey. World trade is 0.3 million tons lower at 208.7 million as reduced exports for Russia and Ukraine more than offset increases for Australia and Argentina. Russia and Ukraine exports are each lowered 1.0 million tons to 39.0 and 11.5 million, respectively. The conflict in the Black Sea region continues to reduce shipments to date for both countries below their long-term averages despite efforts to utilize alternative ports and transportation modes. Projected 2026/27 global ending stocks are 4.8 million tons higher at 280.8 million tons, largely on increases for Ukraine, Argentina, Russia, and Kazakhstan. COARSE GRAINS: This month’s 2026/27 U.S. corn outlook is for smaller production, domestic use, exports, and ending stocks. Corn production is forecast at 15.9 billion bushels, down 141 million from last month with a 1.6-bushel reduction in yield to 179.6 bushels per acre. Harvested area for grain remains at 88.5 million acres. With a smaller crop, feed and residual use is lowered 25 million bushels to 6.0 billion. Exports are also lowered this month, down 25 million bushels to 3.3 billion reflecting shipments and sales to date. Corn ending stocks are reduced to 1.8 billion bushels. The season-average corn price received by producers is lowered $0.10 per bushel to $4.60. This month’s 2026/27 global coarse grain outlook is for nearly unchanged production, increased trade, lower consumption, and larger ending stocks relative to last month. Global coarse grain output is projected at 1.589 billion tons with gains in Argentina, Brazil, and Australia offset by declines in the United States and South Africa. Corn production in Argentina is raised 2.0 million tons to 57.0 million on above average planting progress that indicates expanded area. In Brazil, WASDE-677-2 corn production is raised 2.0 million tons to 141.0 million on larger first crop corn area and sorghum production is raised 1.5 million tons to 8.0 million, also on expanded area. Mexico’s corn production is lowered due to higher rates of area abandonment in the south. Global barley production is increased 1.0 million tons to 161.1 million, mostly reflecting gains for Australia and Ukraine that are partly offset by lower output for Russia. Global coarse grain trade rose 1.5 million tons this month to 255.4 million. Barley and sorghum trade are higher this month on increased barley exports from Australia and larger sorghum shipments from Brazil. Global corn trade is reduced as lower exports for Ukraine, the United States, and India more than offset an increase for Argentina. Global coarse grain use is lower at 1.613 billion tons. In China, lower corn feed use is largely offset by increased barley and sorghum consumption. In Brazil, corn use is higher with increased feed use. Coarse grain use also is reduced for the EU, Australia, Russia, Mexico, China, and Turkey. Global coarse grain ending stocks are raised 2.9 million tons to 316.2 million, primarily reflecting larger corn inventories for Ukraine and China. RICE: The outlook for 2026/27 U.S. rice this month is for slightly smaller supplies, unchanged domestic use and exports, and marginally lower ending stocks. Supplies are reduced, all on production, which is decreased to 157.6 million cwt, all on lower yields as indicated by the NASS November Crop Production report. The average all-rice yield is down 4 pounds per acre to 7,664 pounds. All-rice 2026/27 ending stocks are projected 0.1 million cwt lower at 39.9 million. The 2026/27 all-rice season-average farm price (SAFP) is projected down $0.10 per cwt at $15.10, all on a reduced California SAFP for medium- and short-grain. The 2026/27 global rice outlook this month is for higher supplies, reduced consumption and trade, and increased ending stocks. Global supplies are raised 0.7 million tons to 738.0 million on higher beginning stocks for Nigeria, Bangladesh, and Thailand combined with greater production for Argentina and Australia. World 2026/27 consumption is decreased 0.6 million tons to 538.2 million, on reductions for Bangladesh and several African countries. Global 2026/27 trade is 1.1 million tons lower at 60.2 million tons, mainly on reduced India exports. Projected 2026/27 world ending stocks are raised 1.3 million tons to 199.8 million, largely on higher stocks for India. OILSEEDS: The U.S. soybean outlook for 2026/27 shows increased production and exports, and lower ending stocks. Soybean production is forecast at 4.6 billion bushels, up 12 million, as higher yields in Minnesota, Iowa, South Dakota, and Nebraska account for most of the change in production. Partly offsetting is lower production for Illinois, Kansas, Missouri, and Arkansas. Soybean exports are raised 25 million bushels based on larger supplies and early season export pace. With slightly higher seed use and unchanged crush, soybean ending stocks are lowered 15 million bushels to 300 million. The U.S. season-average soybean price for 2026/27 is forecast at $12.10 per bushel, up 10 cents from last month. The soybean meal price is forecast unchanged at $340 per short ton. The soybean oil price is lowered 2 cents to 68 cents per pound. Global oilseed production for 2026/27 is projected at 721.9 million tons, down 0.3 million from last month. Lower soybean, sunflowerseed, peanut, and cottonseed production is partly offset by higher rapeseed output. Global rapeseed production is raised 0.7 million tons on higher yields for Australia and Uruguay. Global sunflowerseed production is reduced 0.5 million tons on lower production for Russia and Argentina. Partly offsetting is higher production for South Africa on an increase to area harvested. The global soybean supply and demand forecasts for 2026/27 include lower production, higher crush and trade, and lower ending stocks. Global soybean production is reduced 0.2 million tons WASDE-677-3 to 442.5 million, mainly on lower production for Ukraine, South Africa, the EU, and Mexico. Partly offsetting is higher production for the United States and Colombia. Global soybean crush and import demand is raised mainly for Algeria, Colombia, Thailand, and Japan. Soybean exports are raised for the United States and Argentina. Partly offsetting are reduced exports for Ukraine, Uruguay, and South Africa. Global soybean ending stocks are reduced 0.8 million tons to 123.5 million, mainly on lower stocks for the United States and Argentina. SUGAR: U.S. sugar supply and use for 2025/26 are updated with final year data from Sweetener Market Data (SMD). Beet sugar production increased 39,104 short tons, raw value (STRV) to 5.077 million on stronger realized August-September production. Cane sugar production increased 3,234 STRV to 4.245 million on increased September sugar production in Louisiana. Imports decreased 35,166 STRV to 3.062 million, reflecting final high-tier tariff imports recorded by Customs. Deliveries for human consumption decreased 95,753 STRV to 12.296 million. Deliveries had been declining year-over-year starting in the summer and the trend accelerated going into September. July-September deliveries were recorded at 3.082 million, down from 3.327 million for the same period last year. For the year, deliveries from domestic processors and refiners increased 1.2 percent relative to last year to 11.756 million STRV while direct consumption imports decreased 28.5 percent to only 540,093 STRV. With lower prices, beet sugar deliveries were up 4.4 percent over last year while cane sugar deliveries were down 1.2 percent. Ending stocks totaled 2.199 million STRV for an ending stocks-to-use ratio of 17.51 percent. Ending beet sugar stocks decreased 28.3 percent from last year’s elevated level to 852,824 STRV on the strong beet sugar delivery pace. Cane sugar stocks held by refiners and processors remained historically elevated at 1.346 million STRV. U.S. sugar supply for 2026/27 is decreased to 14.149 million STRV as lower beginning stocks and production are only partially offset by increased imports. Beet sugar production is lowered 189,852 STRV to 4.552 million on lower national sugarbeet production forecast by USDA/NASS and the August-September adjustment referred to above. Florida sugar production is reduced 58,007 STRV to 1.757 million based on a reduction in sugarcane yield forecast by NASS. Imports are increased 42,743 STRV on higher high-tier tariff imports to 3.430 million. Projected refined high-tier imports are carried over from 2025/26 at 509,737 STRV, a slight increase over last month. High-tier raw imports are recorded when entered and amount to 41,311 STRV so far. Projected deliveries for human consumption are carried over from 2025/26 at 12.296 million STRV and other deliveries are projected at 105,000 STRV. Ending stocks are residually projected at 1.723 million STRV for an ending stocks-to-use ratio of 13.87 percent, down from 14.90 percent last month. Mexico sugar production for 2026/27 is decreased from last month by 84,906 metric tons (MT) to 5.292 million. Yields had been already forecast low due to rises in fertilizer prices and other input costs. Emerging pest pressures were largely responsible for this month’s reduction with infestations particularly strong in Campeche (90 percent of area) and also in Huasteca and Pánuco (30 percent of area). The harvest has not yet started, but worries abound over unseasonal rainfall at the harvest start delaying production and diluting sucrose levels and also reducing yields. Based on FAS Post reporting, domestic deliveries for human consumption are lowered 42,453 MT but are offset by increased deliveries into the IMMEX program. The Post also advises that significant exports to the United States of sugar lower than 99.2 polarity will not take place until mid-December when that sugar is produced. Notably, low polarity sugar thought to be in inventory is not readily available for export for the first quarter of 2026/27. LIVESTOCK, POULTRY, AND DAIRY: The forecast for 2026 red meat and poultry production is lowered from last month as lower beef and broiler production more than offsets increased pork and turkey production. Beef production is lowered in the fourth quarter on a slower pace of fed WASDE-677-4 cattle marketings and lighter dressed weights. Pork production is raised on a larger-than-expected supply of market-ready hogs in the fourth quarter. Broiler production is lowered on current slaughter and placement data. Turkey production is increased on heavier weights and a higher share of toms being slaughtered than is seasonally typical. Egg production is increased on growth in the layer flock. For 2027, the red meat and poultry production forecast is reduced as lower beef and broiler production more than offsets higher turkey production. Beef production is lowered on lower-than-expected placements in the second half of 2026 that are expected to reduce marketings in the first half of 2027. Pork production is unchanged. Broiler production is lowered on lower seasonal slaughter in the second half of the year. Turkey production is raised on hatchery data and heavier weights. Egg production is increased on higher seasonal production, primarily in the second half of the year. Beef imports are raised for 2026 and the second half of 2027 on continued strength in U.S. beef demand and lower domestic production. Beef exports are raised slightly in both 2026 and 2027 on strong demand in East Asian markets. Pork exports are lowered for both 2026 and 2027 on weakness in shipments to Western Hemisphere markets and increased global competition. Broiler exports are raised for 2026 on continued strength in shipments to the Caribbean but are unchanged for 2027. Turkey export forecasts are unchanged for 2026 and 2027. Egg exports are increased for 2026 and 2027 on expected strength in shipments to the Caribbean. Cattle price forecasts are lowered for 2026 on recent weakness in prices due to a readily available supply of market-ready cattle in the fourth quarter. For 2027, cattle prices are unchanged. Hog price forecasts are decreased for the remainder of 2026 and the first half of 2027 on persistent weakness in the pork cutout and on increased production. Broiler prices are raised for the fourth quarter of 2026 on recent price strength but prices are unchanged for 2027. Turkey price forecasts for 2026 and 2027 are raised on recent price strength and tight supplies of whole birds. Egg price forecasts are lowered for the remainder of 2026 and 2027 on recent price weakness and increased production. The milk production forecast is increased for both 2026 and 2027. Based on the latest Milk Production report, both milk cow inventories and output per cow are raised for 2026 and 2027. Strong milk prices and supplemental income from calf sales are expected to continue to support herd growth. For 2026 and 2027, commercial imports on a fat basis are unchanged while commercial imports on a skim solids basis are lowered to reflect expected reduced shipments of yogurt, whey, and milk proteins. For both years, commercial exports on a skim solids basis are raised to reflect the continued strength of exports of nonfat dry milk (NDM), whey protein concentrates, and lactose exports. For 2026, commercial exports on a fat basis are reduced due to lower-than-expected butter exports in the third quarter. For 2027, commercial exports on a fat basis are raised as butter exports are expected to increase due to increased price competitiveness. For 2026 and 2027, the price forecasts for both NDM and whey are increased to reflect the recent price strength and continued strength of consumer demand for high protein products. The price forecast for butter is lowered for both 2026 and 2027 due to growth in the milkfat supply outpacing demand. The cheese price is raised for 2026 based on recent price strength but lowered for 2027 due to expected continued supply growth. For both 2026 and 2027, the Class IV price forecast is raised as higher NDM prices more than fully offset the effect of lower butter prices. For 2026, the Class III price is raised on higher WASDE-677-5 cheese and whey prices. In 2027, the Class III price is lowered as higher whey prices only partially offset lower cheese prices. The all milk price forecast is raised to $20.10 per cwt for 2026 and $20.50 per cwt for 2027. COTTON: The U.S. cotton balance sheet for 2026/27 shows lower production, consumption, and ending stocks relative to last month. Projected exports, imports, and beginning stocks are unchanged. The production forecast is reduced by 267,000 bales to 13.18 million as the national average all-cotton yield is lowered by 2 percent to 775 pounds per harvested acre. Upland cotton in the Southwest accounts for 250,000 bales of the overall reduction as the regional average yield is lowered by 5 percent. The production and yield forecasts for the Southeast and West are slightly lower while being marginally higher in the Delta. The forecast for consumption is reduced by 100,000 bales to 1.4 million bales as mill use through September is running behind last year’s already weak pace. With these changes, projected ending stocks are reduced by 100,000 bales to 3.70 million, for a stocks-to-use ratio of 27.0 percent, down from 27.5 percent last month. The projected season-average farm price for upland cotton is lowered by 1 cent from last month to 76 cents/lb. The 2026/27 world cotton balance sheet shows lower production, consumption, and beginning and ending stocks compared to October, while trade is increased slightly. Production is lowered by over 470,000 bales to 117.5 million driven by reductions for India, Turkey, Argentina, and the United States that more than offset larger crops in Australia and China. Consumption is lowered by 335,000 bales to 122.7 million, driven primarily by reductions for Bangladesh and the United States. Projected exports are up by 110,000 bales as higher exports from Cote d’Ivoire and Egypt more than offset a reduction for Kyrgyzstan. Global ending stocks are lowered by just over 110,000 bales as reductions for Argentina, the United States and minor exporters more than offset an increase for Australia, with changes for China and India offsetting one another.
