# Oil rises after Iran says no nuclear material will leave the country

Published Tue, Nov 10 2026 10:13 PM EST Updated Wed, Nov 11 2026 4:19 PM EST

Oil prices rose on Wednesday after falling to two-month lows in the previous session, as the market swung between hopes for a de-escalation in the Iran war and concerns about ongoing attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

Eli Hartman | Bloomberg | Getty Images

Oil prices rose on Wednesday after dropping to two-month lows in the ​previous session, after Iran's security chief ​said Tehran would not allow any ​enriched nuclear material to leave the country.

Washington has said nuclear matters must be part of any initial negotiations with Tehran aimed at ending the US-Israeli war on Iran.

In the previous session, oil ⁠prices initially fell by more than $3 a barrel after US officials played down the need for further military strikes against Iran. Prices later pared some losses after an Iranian security official said no talks were taking place between the two sides.

"Oil prices are being supported by fading ‌hopes of an imminent de-escalation," UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo said.

There is little sign of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz easing after 37 ships were attacked in October, the highest monthly total ​since the start of the war, according to data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy.

"There has been an increase of vessel attacks in the Gulf shipping lanes amidst uncertainty of the diplomacy talks," said June Goh, senior analyst at Sparta Commodities.

She added that the key driver for the ⁠market was whether Iran would engage with US demands to reach a diplomatic agreement.

Brent and WTI prices have fallen about 21% and ‌17%, respectively, since the start of the month on hopes of an end ‌to the war.

"Even in the event a deal is struck, an increase in flows through the waterway would still ⁠depend on mine clearance, insurance costs and whether or not shipowners would even be willing to ⁠take the risk," LSEG Oil Research said in a November 10 note.

On the supply side, US crude stocks rose by 9 million barrels in the latest week, market sources said, ​citing data from the American Petroleum Institute. Official data is ‌due later on Wednesday.
