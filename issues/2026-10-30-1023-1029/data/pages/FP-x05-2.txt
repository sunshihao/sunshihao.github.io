CARACAS, Venezuela (AP) — Following her return to Venezuela, opposition leader María Corina Machado said on Thursday in her first public statements that the transition in the South American country is moving irreversibly toward a presidential election in 2027, while denying any contact with interim president Delcy Rodríguez.

Machado arrived at a university campus in Caracas where, amid anticipation after 10 months in exile, she reiterated that her priority in this process will be “the complete dismantling of the repressive system and the release of all our political prisoners.”

Machado won the opposition’s presidential primary with more than 90% of the vote in the lead-up to 2024 presidential elections. But ruling party loyalists who control the country’s judiciary kept her from appearing on the ballot, which forced her to throw her support behind former diplomat Edmundo González. 

A count by the opposition showed González won by a wide margin, but electoral authorities allied with former President Nicolás Maduro declared him the winner. The U.S. recognized González as the winner and later captured Maduro to face drug trafficking charges in a Jan. 3 raid.

The politician’s return on Sunday occurred during a climate fraught with doubts about Venezuela’s democratic future and the role of the United States. The leader said she would maintain a strong relationship with the administration of U.S. President Donald Trump, who she presented with her Nobel Peace Prize in a symbolic act this year. 

“Thanks to his actions on Jan. 3, the door to freedom has been opened, which will allow Venezuela to move toward a democratic transition and consolidate our country as the greatest ally the United States has ever had in our region,” Machado said. 

Machado believes that the three phases presented by U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio for political change — stabilization, recovery and transition — continue to move toward “holding presidential elections in 2027.”
