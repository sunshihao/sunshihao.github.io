Oil price today: WTI, Brent rise
Oil prices rise as U.S. reportedly tells Gulf countries to prepare for Iran strikes after midterm elections
Published Wed, Oct 28 2026
Key Points
- Crude oil prices climbed Wednesday, as the U.S. is reportedly preparing to renew strikes against Iran after the midterm elections.
Crude oil prices climbed Wednesday, as the U.S. is reportedly preparing to renew strikes against Iran after the midterm elections.
International benchmark Brent crude futures rose 1.3% to close at $115.32 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures gained 2.9% to settle at $102.78 per barrel.
The U.S. has told several Gulf Arab countries to be ready for a short but intense military campaign after the midterms, people briefed on the conversations told The Wall Street Journal.
President Donald Trump, meanwhile, said that the war with Iran would end on his terms, while insisting that Tehran must be prevented from acquiring nuclear weapons.
In the Middle East, Iran-allied Houthi militants said Tuesday they had attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery with a ballistic missile, raising worries about further disruptions to oil supplies.
Concerns over disruptions to oil flows have kept a geopolitical risk premium embedded in crude prices, with traders closely monitoring developments in the region.
