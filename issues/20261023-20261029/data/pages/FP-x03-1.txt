 By HANNA ARHIROVA and VOLODYMYR YURCHUK

KHERSON, Ukraine (AP) — Humanitarian aid is poised to roll into the stricken city of Oleshky, negotiators said Thursday, as a localized ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine stretched into a third day and military units cleared landmines.

Moscow and Kyiv have observed the truce in and around the devastated southern Ukrainian city since Tuesday, but remain otherwise locked in the war that began with Russia’s full-scale invasion more than 4 ½ years ago. Previous ceasefire talks either failed or the truces quickly fell apart.

“Demining of the designated routes has been completed, and the humanitarian convoy has been assembled and is ready,” ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets, who heads Ukraine’s negotiating team, said Thursday. He said aid deliveries were due to begin Friday.

Elsewhere, deadly attacks continued and Ukraine’s central bank warned that damage to industrial targets could push the country into a recession. 

A jet-powered Russian drone hit two passenger trains at a station in Dnipro in central Ukraine on Thursday, killing at least 14 people and injuring 14 more, including a 40-year-old man whose legs had to be amputated, officials said.

Also, a Russian cruise missile killed two 15-year-old girls in the northern city of Chernihiv, and three civilians died when a Russian drone hit an apartment block in Kremenchuk in central Ukraine, authorities said. Fifty-six people were injured in the strikes, including 10 children.
