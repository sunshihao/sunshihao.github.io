AL-TUFF, Syria (AP) — Jordan and Syria agreed Tuesday to form a joint committee to investigate the circumstances leading to Jordanian strikes that killed a number of civilians in Syria the day before, raising tensions between the neighboring countries.

Jordan on Tuesday said its airstrikes over Syria killed some 20 militants of the extremist Islamic State group, but acknowledged that civilians had also been killed in the attacks. Syrian authorities have reported seven people killed in the strikes, most of them women and children.

A joint statement after a call between the Syrian and Jordanian foreign ministers said the two countries will “develop mechanisms for field and intelligence coordination to deal with terrorist threats in a manner that ensures the preservation of Syrian sovereignty and the protection of the lives of innocent civilians.”

The Hashemite Kingdom’s air force on Monday evening carried out airstrikes in Syria in retaliation for last week’s attacks on a funeral at a Greek Orthodox Church in the southern Jordanian city of Karak that killed nine people. Earlier that day, it announced it arrested the three gunmen, but did not disclose their affiliation.
