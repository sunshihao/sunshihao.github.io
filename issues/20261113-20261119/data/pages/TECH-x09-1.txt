TARRYTOWN, N.Y.

Nov. 18, 2026

 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- 

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, Inc.

 (NASDAQ: REGN) today announced that the 

U.S. Food and Drug Administration

FDA

) has approved Livsayis™ (cemdisiran) for the treatment of generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) in adult patients who are anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody-positive, the predominant form of the disease. Livsayis is a novel, complement component factor 5 (C5)-directed small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapeutic that reduces levels of circulating C5 protein, limiting terminal complement activation, a key driver of the disease. It is the first and only approved siRNA therapy in gMG and the only 

FDA

-approved gMG treatment administered subcutaneously four times a year.

“For people living with gMG, every day can be unpredictable, due to fluctuating muscle weakness and fatigue. They may also experience severe exacerbations that can be life-threatening. Many still experience suboptimal disease control, whether from waning of therapeutic effect, inadequate response or high treatment burden,” said 

Tuan Vu

, M.D., Professor of Neurology at the 

University of South Florida Morsani College of Medicine

, Division Director for 

Neuromuscular Medicine and EMG Laboratories

, and a global principal investigator of the NIMBLE trial. “In the Phase 3 NIMBLE trial, Livsayis delivered rapid, deep disease control – maintained through the end of dosing intervals – and offers a four-times-a-year subcutaneous dosing schedule. These results highlight how a first-of-its-kind approach can address a key driver of disease and provide a compelling treatment option.”

FDA

 approval was based on results from the Phase 3 NIMBLE trial. Livsayis demonstrated the following least square mean change from baseline:

- 4.5-point improvement on the Myasthenia Gravis-Activities of Daily Living (MG-ADL) total score at 24 weeks (0.4 standard error [SE]) compared to a 2.2-point improvement with placebo (0.5 SE). This corresponded to a placebo-adjusted improvement of 2.3 points (p=0.0005). MG-ADL is a clinician-administered patient-reported assessment of daily functions impacted by gMG, such as talking, chewing, swallowing, breathing, vision and mobility.
