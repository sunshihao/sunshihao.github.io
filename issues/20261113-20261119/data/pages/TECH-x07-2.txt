# Starship returns to Starbase and a successful tower catch
**[Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/news-archive/?by=Spaceflight%20Now)** November 13, 2026 7:15 p.m.
Starship descends into the waiting arms of the launch pad tower at Starbase. Image: Adam Bernstein/Spaceflight Now.

...

SpaceX flew it’s most ambitious Starship flight to date, hoping to demonstrate its revolutionary rocket is fully reusable by recovering both its first and second stages, but although the Ship upper stage reached orbit, deployed 26 Starlink satellites and successfully returned to Starbase, the booster diverted to a fiery splashdown off shore in the Gulf.
The catch occurred at 3:20 p.m. CST (2120 UTC) after Ship 42 reentered the atmosphere over the Pacific Ocean and made a supersonic flight across Mexico and the South West United States before turning out over the ocean and lining itself up for a catch at Staircase’s pad 2.
It was the first time the upper stage of an orbital launch vehicle has returned to Earth and a crucial step to making Starship a fully reusable rocket.

...

The integrated Starship vehicle had lifted off from Starbase in Texas at 4:47 p.m. CST (5:47 p.m. EST / 2247 UTC) on Friday, Nov. 13.
The flight plan called for Super Heavy Booster 22 to return to pad 2 at Starbase about 6.5 minutes after launching from there but it diverted to a splashdown offshore.
Starship lifts off on its 15th flight from Starbase, Texas at 5:48 p.m. CST on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026. Image: Adam Bernstein/Spaceflight Now. Starship lifts off on its 15th flight from Starbase, Texas at 4:48 p.m. CST on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026. Image: Adam Bernstein/Spaceflight Now.
“GNC [Guidance, Navigation and Control] triggered that abort. It was doing what it was supposed to do, basically detected that the booster was not in the place that it was supposed to be to proceed with the catch, did an automated abort, and then headed out to that offshore splashdown location,” SpaceX commentator Dan Huot said in an update about 16.5 hours into the mission.

...

The vehicle exploded and disappeared from view before appearing to rise back out of the sea as remaining propellants burned off. Three previous boosters were caught by the launch pad tower but Version 3 of the Super Heavy is yet to make a successful return.
