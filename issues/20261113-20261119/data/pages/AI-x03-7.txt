# APEC live: China releases leaders' statement after its disappearance from APEC website

'Shenzhen Outlook' document affirms need to improve trade environment

Presidents Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Donald Trump held a historic trilateral meeting at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Nov. 19, on the sidelines of the APEC forum. © Reuters

WATARU SUZUKI, KENJI KAWASE and PAK YIU

November 17, 2026 10:54 JST Updated on November 19, 2026 23:55 JST

SHENZHEN -- Chinese President Xi Jinping hosted leaders including U.S. counterpart Donald Trump and Russia's Vladimir Putin at this year's Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, showcasing his own country's technological prowess against a backdrop of cold and hot conflicts.

The proceedings in the southern tech hub of Shenzhen kicked off Tuesday with a CEO Summit of business leaders, followed by an Economic Leaders' Meeting that wrapped up on Thursday.

On the sidelines, Xi and Trump had their third summit of the year as they sought to manage a fraught superpower rivalry. The biggest moment was a historic trilateral summit among Xi, Trump and Putin, where they discussed nuclear arms and other issues.

Nikkei Asia is on the ground in Shenzhen to cover the meetings.

Here are the latest developments (Shenzhen time):

Thursday, Nov. 19

10:47 p.m. The APEC leaders' "Shenzhen Outlook" statement is still missing from the official website. However, China's state news agency Xinhua has published the statement on its news site, with the Chinese version released at 9:18 p.m. and the English one at 9:50 p.m.

"The 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Economic Leaders' Meeting adopted 2026 APEC Leaders' Shenzhen Outlook on Thursday," according to the Xinhua readout.

Shortly after, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi appeared on stage for a news conference.

The APEC secretariat did not respond to a request for comment.

8:30 p.m. Stan Shih, founder of computer maker Acer and Taiwan's representative at the APEC summit, told reporters upon returning to Taipei that he reiterated that the technology-oriented island democracy was an "indispensable partner in the progress of global AI."

After reading out his prepared remarks at Taoyuan International Airport, the tech veteran offered some personal thoughts on his trip to Shenzhen.

"I am extremely not used to it," he said, referring to having to stick to a script. "Being a political leader is so unfree," he said. "I wasn't nervous but I was definitely under pressure."

7:58 p.m. The joint statement by APEC leaders released earlier has been taken down from the official website. Originally published as the "Shenzhen Outlook," the online site shows only "404" now, indicating that the page was not found.

Nikkei Asia is reaching out to the APEC secretariat for comment.

6:55 p.m. A statement has been published from the trilateral between Trump, Xi and Putin.
