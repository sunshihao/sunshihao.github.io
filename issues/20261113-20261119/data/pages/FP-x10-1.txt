# For the first time, UN's highest court finds a country responsible for genocide: Myanmar
URL: https://apnews.com/article/rohingya-myanmar-genocide-court-icj-30df77b12bf3f911f1100e77ebe3973a
Published: 2026-11-19
Author: Mike Corder and Molly Quell

THE HAGUE, Netherlands (AP) — In a landmark judgment, the United Nations’ top court ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority in a brutal 2017 military operation and ordered the Southeast Asian nation to allow people who fled the violence to return home.

The decision marks the first time the court has ruled that a state committed genocide and comes as the World Court also is handling genocide allegations linked to the Gaza conflict and the war in Ukraine.

But it remains to be seen how much of the ruling can be implemented. The court’s rulings are final and legally binding, but it has no way to enforce them beyond reporting noncompliance to the U.N. Security Council.

International Court of Justice President Yuji Iwasawa said the court, in a 9-6 decision, found that Myanmar “has committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine state, in violation of its obligations under the Genocide Convention.”

The 15-judge panel of the World Court, which included Navi Pillay, the respected international jurist who won the Nobel Peace Prize last month, rejected Myanmar’s defense that the 2017 campaign was a legitimate operation against insurgents.

Iwasawa said that “the court finds that Myanmar’s security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians, perpetrated widespread acts of sexual violence, as well as acts of extreme violence against children, and attacked fleeing Rohingya as they sought to flee in large numbers.”

The atmosphere following the hearing was electric, with lawyers and members of the Rohingya, some of whom had traveled thousands of miles to attend the reading, hugging and crying.

“I’m beyond ecstatic today, hearing the judgment, being read out in detail. A lot of those moments and instances that the judges really went into detail, it really validates the kind of experience that Rohingya have,” said Yasmin Ullah, a Rohingya rights activist who sat in the court’s ornate, wood-paneled Great Hall of Justice for the nearly three-hour reading of the ruling.

In Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, refugee Mohammad Rofik welcomed the recognition of the plight of the Rohingya.

“Today, the world could recognize and give the victory to the Rohingya community, not only the Rohingya community. It is the victory of the whole community of ... Myanmar," he said.

There was no immediate reaction from the Myanmar delegation in The Hague or from the government in Myanmar.

The court also ordered Myanmar to commit to reparations including allowing Rohingya “safe, voluntary and dignified return to their place of residence.” It ordered Myanmar to investigate, prosecute and punish people found to have committed genocidal acts and to pay compensation to Rohingya. The amount will be decided in later court proceedings.
