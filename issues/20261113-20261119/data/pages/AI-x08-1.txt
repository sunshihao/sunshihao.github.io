# Quaise Energy Signs 50 MW Geothermal Power Purchase Agreement with Google

 Nov. 17, 2026 

Agreement advances development of commercial-scale superhot geothermal, will supply carbon-free energy to Oregon communities as early as 2030

 Project Obsidian in Central Oregon 

BEND, Ore. — November 17, 2026 — Quaise Energy, a leading developer of utility-scale superhot geothermal energy, today announced that Google has signed a power purchase agreement (PPA) for 50 megawatts (MW) of superhot geothermal power from Project Obsidian, Quaise's flagship project in Central Oregon. Once online, this always-on, carbon-free energy is slated to support Google's existing data center operations in The Dalles, Ore. and the region’s communities. The agreement will help catalyze the commercial development of superhot geothermal energy, providing benefits for ratepayers and helping protect the environment.

Beginning as early as 2030, Google will purchase 50 MW of power from Project Obsidian. The initial phase of development will use conventional drilling technology to access superhot geothermal conditions at commercial scale. Superhot geothermal is a virtually limitless energy source that uses the Earth’s natural heat to generate up to 10x more clean power per well than traditional geothermal systems. The second phase of development at Project Obsidian is expected to be the first in the world to use millimeter wave drilling, a technology designed to uniquely access superhot geothermal conditions worldwide. 

“We started Quaise to make the Earth's most abundant energy source universally accessible — and this agreement marks a critical next step, not just for Quaise but for the entire clean energy sector," said Carlos Araque, CEO and President of Quaise Energy. “Together with Google, we are building the commercial foundation for superhot geothermal and cementing the United States as the global leader in geothermal power.”

Unlike traditional geothermal energy, which is limited to regions with naturally occurring hot water reservoirs rarely exceeding 600°F, Quaise develops Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS) at superhot conditions, which are expected to enable utility-scale projects to compete with today’s most efficient power plants. Quaise is simultaneously advancing the development of millimeter wave drilling, a technology that can plug into existing drilling rigs to economically reach superhot geothermal conditions.
