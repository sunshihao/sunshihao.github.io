# U.S. natgas futures hit nearly five-month high at $3.38 as colder forecasts boost heating demand 

 11/19/2026 

 U.S. natural gas futures hit their highest since June 26 as colder forecasts lifted heating demand expectations ahead of the EIA's weekly storage report. 

(P&GJ) — U.S. natural gas futures hit nearly a five-month high Nov. 19, as forecasts for colder weather across key consuming regions boosted expectations for heating demand ahead of a weekly storage report, according to Reuters.

Front-month gas futures for December delivery on the New York Mercantile Exchange traded 1.1 cents, or 0.3%, higher at $3.38/MMBtu at 9:21 a.m. ET, having earlier hit their highest level since June 26.

"I think the market has increasingly begun to recognize the potential for stronger winter heating demand than previously anticipated, which has contributed to the recent rebound in natural gas prices," said Zhen Zhu, managing consultant at C.H. Guernsey and Co.

LSEG's two-week heating degree day outlook rose to 302 from 297 a day earlier, indicating higher expected heating demand over the next 14 days.

Analysts expected the EIA's weekly report to show a 27-Bft³ storage injection for the week ended Nov. 13, which would put inventories at 3,889 Bft³, about 2.3% above normal.

"One important factor to watch is the pace of storage withdrawals — if weekly withdrawals continue to fall below seasonal norms, suggesting a relatively comfortable supply-demand balance, they could place a significant ceiling on price increases," Zhu said.

LSEG said average gas demand in the Lower 48 states, including exports, would ease from 123.9 Bft³d this week to 123.7 Bft³d next week.

European wholesale gas prices rose Nov. 19 because of an outage at Norway's Troll gas field and cooler weather forecasts.
