The EU is expected to agree on 113 new sanctions against Russia, a move Moscow says harms the current Russia-Ukraine peace process.
Reuters reported that the EU was expected to impose a new round of up to 113 sanctions against Russia later on Monday (16 November), including measures targeting Russian individuals accused of involvement in cyberattacks and sabotage. The EU hopes to pressure Russia to end its military operations in Ukraine.
The Kremlin responded the same day, saying EU officials’ pledges of continued support for Ukraine, alongside threats to step up pressure on Russia through sanctions, were harmful to the current peace process.
The Kremlin said last week that Russia was willing to allow Europe to participate in Ukraine peace talks if European countries showed a genuine willingness to contribute to peace.
After months of hesitation, EU leaders recently agreed that the UK, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and relevant EU institutions would represent Europe in a new round of talks involving the US, Russia and Ukraine.
Russian officials have also called for a broad agreement on Europe’s security architecture. However, the move could divert attention from the urgent need for a ceasefire.
"What we need to see is a ceasefire to actually start the talks about anything else. We don't see that at the moment because it is (Russia) really escalating," EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said.
