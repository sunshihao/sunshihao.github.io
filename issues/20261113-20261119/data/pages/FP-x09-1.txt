# Commission proposes €45 billion in additional financial support for Ukraine in 2027

- News article
- 18 November 2026
- Directorate-General for Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood
- 7 min read

Today, the European Commission adopted a set of legislative proposals to provide up to €45 billion in additional financial support to Ukraine in 2027, helping to address the country's estimated €70 billion financing gap for next year. These proposals follow the EU's €90 billion Ukraine Support Loan for 2026 and 2027 and reaffirm the Union's continued commitment to supporting Ukraine in the face of Russia's ongoing war of aggression.

President Ursula von der Leyen said: “ Europe will continue to ensure maximum support to Ukraine and maximum pressure on Russia. We have provided vital support to Ukraine throughout this war. And we will continue to help Ukraine protect its people and infrastructure from Russia's attacks. And keep schools and hospitals open as Russia rains drones and missiles on Ukrainian cities. For 2026, Ukraine's needs are covered. And today we are proposing a further €45 billion to cover two thirds of its needs for 2027. Together, with our international partners, we will strengthen Ukraine. For as long as it takes to achieve a just and lasting peace.”

The legislative package consists of:

- A proposal establishing an Instrument for Coordinated Support to Ukraine, providing a new loan of up to €45 billion to be financed by borrowing in capital markets.
- A proposal to amend the current Multiannual Financial Framework Regulation enabling the EU budget to guarantee the new loan.
- Incentives for international partners to provide additional financial support to Ukraine via proposals to amend the existing Ukraine Facility and the Ukraine Loan Cooperation Mechanism.

Predictable financial support for Ukraine in 2027

The proposed €45 billion support will take the form of a limited-recourse loan. This means that Ukraine will be required to repay the principal only once it receives reparations from Russia.

The proposed support would be structured in two components, with up to €25 billion allocated to military assistance and up to €20 billion provided as general budget support. The support will help Ukraine strengthen its defence capabilities, maintain essential public services and sustain its economic resilience. It will also support closer integration between Ukraine's and Europe's defence industries. The budget support will be conditional on Ukraine pursuing reforms, including in the areas of rule of law and anti-corruption which are relevant for the implementation of the interim benchmarks under the Fundamentals Cluster.
