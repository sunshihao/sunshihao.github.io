# Ukraine: EU lists 113 individuals and entities to further restrict Russia’s ability to finance its war of aggression

In response to Russia’s indiscriminate attacks against Ukraine, particularly against civilian infrastructure, the Council today imposed additional restrictive measures on 12 individuals and 101 entities for their role in those economic sectors which provide a substantial source of revenue to Russia’s war of aggression and support its shadow fleet.

Today’s listings include nearly 50 Russian banks and financial institutions. By progressively tightening and expanding sanctions on Russia's banking sector, the EU is cutting off the financial channels that process state revenues and fund Russia’s war effort.

Moreover, the Council is cracking down on Russia’s e-commerce. Today’s package includes the sole shareholder of Russian retail giant Wildberries, and an individual controlling the majority stake in another major Russian e-commerce company, Ozon.

The Council is also listing additional entities and businesspersons in the Russian oil and energy sector. The energy companies listed today manage Russian refineries and the St. Petersburg oil terminal and are active in the exploration and production segment.

Lastly, the Council is targeting Russia’s shadow fleet, by listing various companies from the United Arab Emirates, China, Seychelles and Oman that are buyers of crude oil products of Russian origin, as well as certain businesspersons who own or manage vessels that are subject to EU restrictive measures, and illegally transport Russian oil, while engaging in high-risk shipping activities.

Today’s listings were adopted under the EU's restrictive measures framework targeting actions undermining or threatening Ukraine’s territorial integrity, sovereignty and independence. Those listed under this sanctions regime are subject to an asset freeze and it is prohibited to provide them with funds or economic resources either directly or indirectly or for their benefit. Additionally, a travel ban to the EU applies to the natural persons named in today’s listing.

The relevant legal acts have been published in the Official Journal of the European Union.

## Background 

In its conclusions of 15 and 16 October 2026 the European Council reaffirmed its continued firm and unwavering support for Ukraine’s independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity within its internationally recognised borders. The European Council stated that the EU will continue to provide, in coordination with like-minded partners and allies, comprehensive political, financial, economic, humanitarian, military and diplomatic support to Ukraine and its people.
