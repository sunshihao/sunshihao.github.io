# Brussels prepares €45B loan to Ukraine that it says Russia will repay 

 The proposed loan would cover roughly two-thirds of Ukraine’s €70 billion financing needs for next year. 

 People stand in front of a makeshift memorial for Ukrainian and foreign soldiers in Independence Square in Kyiv on Nov. 16, 2026. | Tetiana Dzhafarova/AFP via Getty Images 

 November 18, 2026 1:05 am CET 

 By Bjarke Smith-Meyer 

BRUSSELS — The European Commission will propose a €45 billion loan to Ukraine that would be repaid using future Russian war reparations, according to a document seen by POLITICO.

If Moscow refuses to pay reparations, “the Union reserves its right to make use of the Russian assets immobilised in the Union to repay the loan,” the document said. That way, Ukraine never has to repay the loan, keeping its mounting debt sustainable after more than four years of war. Brussels will raise the money by issuing common debt, backed by the EU budget.

EU leaders will discuss the loan when they meet in Brussels at the end of next week to discuss the bloc’s next seven-year budget and Kyiv’s financing needs.

The EU executive arm will unveil “the Instrument for Coordinated Support to Ukraine” on Wednesday to cover roughly two-thirds of Ukraine’s €70 billion financing gap for 2027, although payouts could stretch out until the end of 2028. Brussels hopes that the final third will come from international allies, especially Canada, Japan, the U.S., and the U.K.

The next EU budget itself could also help cover the borrowing costs of the loan, which will be subject to reforms that Ukraine must complete to secure the payouts, the document said.

The new financial “instrument” includes many of the hallmarks of the €90 billion loan that leaders agreed in December, including references to using war reparations (or Russian assets) to repay the debt. EU leaders agreed to issue the loan after talks to leverage frozen Russian state assets to finance Ukraine's defense collapsed amid fierce Belgian resistance.

Valdis Dombrovskis attendz a meeting of the Eurogroup in Brussels on Nov. 9, 2026. | Nicolas Tucat/AFP via Getty Images

But this time, the Commission wants no carve-outs for Czechia, Hungary, and Slovakia.

“We are putting the emphasis of importance of ensuring that it is all 27 EU Member States which are contributing to this program, not 24, as it is right now,” Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis told reporters on the margins of Tuesday’s growth forecast.

The document also suggested new measures that could trigger fresh loans from G7 countries, to be eventually “repaid from future flows of extraordinary revenues stemming from the immobilisation of Russian sovereign assets.”

Other provisions would allow foreign countries with high creditworthiness to provide financial guarantees to the EU that could mobilize another €5 billion in loans.
