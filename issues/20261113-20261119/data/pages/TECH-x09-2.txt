# Alnylam Collaborator Regeneron Secures FDA Approval of Livsayis™ (cemdisiran), the Seventh FDA-Approved Therapeutic from Alnylam's RNAi Platform in Less Than Nine Years

Nov 19, 2026

 − First RNAi Therapeutic Approved for Generalized Myasthenia Gravis – 

 − Alnylam to Receive $25 Million Payment from Regeneron for Approval; Eligible to Receive Tiered, Double-Digit Royalties (Up to 15%) on Global Net Sales − 

CAMBRIDGE, Mass.--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Nasdaq: ALNY), the leading RNAi therapeutics company, announced that the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved Livsayis™ (cemdisiran), an Alnylam-discovered RNAi therapeutic that was developed and will be commercialized by Regeneron, for the treatment of generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) in adult patients who are anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody-positive. This marks the seventh Alnylam-discovered RNAi therapeutic to receive FDA approval in less than nine years and the first RNAi therapeutic approved for gMG.

Livsayis was discovered by Alnylam scientists and advanced into Phase 2 development by Alnylam before becoming part of an RNAi collaboration with Regeneron. In 2024, Regeneron assumed sole responsibility for development, manufacturing and commercialization of the molecule, advancing late-stage clinical development and achieving the FDA approval for Livsayis. Per the agreement with Regeneron, Alnylam will receive a $25 million payment for the approval, and is eligible to receive tiered, double-digit royalties of up to 15 percent on global net sales.

“Seven FDA-approved medicines originating from Alnylam in under nine years is powerful evidence that RNAi has become an important and differentiated platform for creating new medicines,” said Yvonne Greenstreet, M.D., Chief Executive Officer of Alnylam. “Livsayis began with Alnylam science, and its approval demonstrates how our innovation can ultimately reach patients through both our own portfolio and strategic partnerships. As we look ahead, we remain focused on advancing RNAi science, expanding its application to new areas of medicine, and delivering meaningful outcomes for patients.”

Generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) is a rare, chronic autoimmune disease characterized by debilitating and potentially life-threatening muscle weakness. In anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody-positive gMG, activation of the complement system contributes to damage at the neuromuscular junction. Livsayis is an RNAi therapeutic, also known as an siRNA medicine, designed to reduce production of complement component 5 (C5) by silencing the mRNA that encodes the protein. Livsayis is administered subcutaneously four times a year. For additional information about Livsayis, see full Prescribing Information.
