# Senior Officials Map Integration, Connectivity and AI Agenda for Leaders

 The Concluding Senior Officials’ Meeting Shenzhen, People's Republic of China | 16 November 2026 

APEC senior officials met in Shenzhen over the weekend to advance work on trade integration, connectivity and artificial intelligence ahead of the APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting later this week.

Opening the two-day Concluding Senior Officials’ Meeting, Ambassador Chen Xu, Chair of the 2026 APEC Senior Officials’ Meeting said APEC economies had shown they could find common ground despite economic challenges.

“The series of sectoral ministerial meetings and high-level events yielded fruitful outcomes, most of which represent consensus-based deliverables,” said Ambassador Chen, citing agreements reached by APEC ministers in areas from energy and food security to human resources development, transport and finance.

Among those initiatives is China’s proposed statement on the digital economy and artificial intelligence (AI) for people’s well-being, which calls for wider use of the technology in sectors including health care, education and energy. It also seeks to promote safe and trusted AI developmechennt and prevent wider digital divides.

Senior officials stressed that AI’s benefits must be broadly shared as economies look to technology to strengthen productivity and growth.

On trade, officials considered China’s proposed Trade Digitalization Cooperation Framework for 2027–2030 and a draft statement on the Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific, APEC’s long-term goal for regional economic integration.

The proposed FTAAP statement sets out future work on policy research, capacity building and technical cooperation to help economies participate more fully in high-standard trade agreements.

APEC’s trade work this year also advanced paperless trade, including an endorsed workplan for the APEC Centre of Excellence for Paperless Trade and cooperation on electronic bills of lading and AI in customs.

Connectivity discussions included a final review of APEC’s Connectivity Blueprint for 2015–2025 and China’s proposal for further work on regional connectivity. 

The APEC Business Advisory Council (ABAC) also emphasized the urgency of keeping trade and investment open, calling on economies to strengthen supply chain resilience and make connectivity infrastructure more sustainable and ready for the digital economy.

Officials reviewed progress under the Aotearoa Plan of Action to help guide regional cooperation over the next five years.

“I hope our discussions today will help us to bridge the gaps further, to achieve broader consensus and contribute to a successful and fruitful APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting,” Ambassador Chen concluded.
