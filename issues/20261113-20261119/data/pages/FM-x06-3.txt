# Longer-dated Treasury yields fall as traders weigh Waller comments, Fed minutes

Yields at the longer end of the Treasury yield curve fell on Wednesday after Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller called for aggressive action against inflation, which he said has been more persistent than he expected.

Investors took to heart recent pledges by Federal Reserve officials to fight back against widespread inflationary pressures in the economy. The benchmark 10-year Treasury Add To Watchlist yield lost almost 4 basis points to 5.064%, while yields on the longer-dated 30-year Treasury Add To Watchlist bond dropped more than 5 basis points to 5.408%.

The 2-year Treasury Add To Watchlist yield, which reacts most to short-term changes in expectations of Fed policy, was up more than 1 basis point at 4.80% after earlier gaining more than 4 basis points to 4.83%. 

One basis point equals 0.01%, and yields and prices move in opposite directions.

"The Fed appears firmly committed to additional tightening, with the debate shifting from whether to raise rates to how dramatically," said Jeffrey Roach, chief economist at LPL Financial. "Monetary policy will likely tighten further in Q1 next year."

The Fed's Waller jolted the market early Wednesday by telling CNBC in a live interview that a half-percentage-point increase in the Fed's benchmark rate is probably necessary at the Dec. 8-9 meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee.

From here, Waller said the Fed needs to reverse the three quarter-point reductions from last year and may need to do more.

"You might need to, like, shock people," Waller told CNBC's Steve Liesman during the " Squawk Box" interview.

"I'm very, very concerned about inflation. It's kind of headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out," he said. "If you really want to get it down, you've got to start taking more aggressive actions."

Traders have now priced in a 95% chance of a quarter-percentage-point rate hike, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool.

At the same time, FOMC minutes from the Fed's Oct. 27–28 meeting, which saw policymakers hold rates at 3.75%–4% in a 10–2 vote, revealed that officials believe more interest rate hikes will be needed to tame inflation.

"Almost all participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labor market, further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate … would likely be appropriate, in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2 percent objective," the meeting summary said.
