# Strong dollar, tighter Fed policy bets leave gold to languish at 14-month low

Published Wed, Nov 18 2026 10:28 PM EST Updated Thu, Nov 19 2026 1:27 PM EST

Gold prices rose on Thursday after hitting a more than one-year low in the previous session, as investors looked to Federal Reserve commentary for clues on the U.S. interest-rate outlook.

Chalinee Thirasupa | Bloomberg | Getty Images

Gold ‌eased to a 14-month low on Thursday, as it continued to face pressure from strength in the U.S. dollar, with markets fully pricing in a rate hike at the Federal Reserve's December meeting owing ​to stubborn inflation.

Spot gold Add To Watchlist was down 1.24% at $3,676.27 per ounce by 1:18 p.m. ​ET, after touching its lowest level since September 2025 earlier ⁠in the session.

U.S. gold futures Add To Watchlist for December delivery fell 1.67% to $3,679.50 per ounce.

"Interest rate expectations ​and a strong dollar are two factors weighing on gold prices today. Minutes from last ​month's FOMC meeting showed almost all Fed officials expected interest rates will need to move higher, and inflation data released since that meeting support further rate increases here in the U.S.," said Chris Gaffney, ​head of World Markets at EverBank.

The dollar index this week reached its highest level since ​April 2, 2025, making greenback-priced commodities like gold more expensive for buyers holding other currencies.

Fed minutes released ‌on ⁠Wednesday showed policymakers in October saw rising inflation risks that may warrant a stronger policy response.

The minutes reinforced sentiments already expressed by several policymakers throughout the week, including Governor Christopher Waller, who said the central bank may need to respond more aggressively to rising prices.

Fed Governor Michael ​Barr said he would ​likely support tighter monetary ⁠policy in an economy still facing inflation pressures.

Traders see a 96.4% chance of a 25-basis-point rate increase in December and a nearly 4% ​probability of a larger 50-basis-point hike, according to CME FedWatch.

Higher interest rates reduce the appeal ​of non-yielding ⁠bullion by raising its opportunity cost.

In the near term, markets would need to see increased demand for gold from both consumers and central banks for prices to recover from the one-year low, ⁠Gaffney said, ​adding, "the recent computer outages, and worries about AI could ​lead investors to turn back toward 'hard assets' such as gold."

Spot silver Add To Watchlist lost 1.26% to $44.45 per ounce, platinum Add To Watchlist fell 0.85% ​to $1,359.60, and palladium fell 3.09% to $918.87.
