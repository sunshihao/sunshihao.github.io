# Xi, Putin blast Western 'protectionism' and sanctions as Trump lands in China

SHENZHEN, China, Nov 18 (Reuters) - China's President Xi Jinping pledged on Wednesday to stand up against trade protectionism, but also gave a warm welcome to US President Donald ​Trump as he arrived ahead of a high-stakes trilateral meeting that will include the Russian leader Vladimir Putin.

Xi greeted Trump at the airport, the first time he had done so with any visiting leader since ‌becoming president, reciprocating his counterpart's gesture at Joint Base Andrews in September when they last met.

Trump's arrival came after Xi and Putin delivered pointed speeches ahead of an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in the southern Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen.

In his opening address, Xi urged support for what he called "free and open trade," trying to portray his country as a stable partner on the global stage, in contrast with the US, which has raised tariffs and taken a volatile approach to foreign policy under Trump.

In his speech, Putin criticised Western sanctions imposed over Russia's war in Ukraine.

At the Shenzhen airport, Xi ​and Trump briefly hugged and exchanged words before walking to their motorcades.

"China is a country of etiquette and has a tradition of reciprocating courtesy," Xi was quoted as saying by state news agency Xinhua. He added he was ​looking forward to discussing China-US relations, Asia-Pacific cooperation and the global landscape.

Trump said every meeting with Xi has been "very pleasant," Xinhua reported.

They then joined Putin and other leaders at the ⁠APEC summit venue for handshakes, a group photo and a banquet.

The three men will meet on the sidelines on Thursday and potentially discuss Russia's war with Ukraine and the US war in Iran, as well as other flashpoints such as trade, AI and nuclear ​weapons.

A US official, and senior Russian officials quoted by Russian news agencies, said Trump would also have a one-on-one meeting with Putin. The Russian officials said no full-scale bilateral negotiations were planned.

Russia's Interfax news agency cited Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov ​as saying that Putin and Xi discussed the trilateral meeting. Russian news agencies also quoted Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov as saying that one hour had been allocated for the three-way meeting.

The trilateral meeting has drawn comparisons with the 1945 Yalta Conference
