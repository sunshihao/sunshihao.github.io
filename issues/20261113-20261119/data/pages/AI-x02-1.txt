# Nvidia posts strong earnings amid Jensen Huang exit, reported SEC probe

- Nathan Bomey

An Nvidia facility in Shenzhen, China. Photo: Cheng Xin/Getty Images

Nvidia posted a stronger-than-expected third quarter and a bullish outlook in its first earnings report after Jensen Huang was ousted as CEO in a scandal that has reportedly spawned an SEC investigation.

- On the company's earnings call, newly appointed chairman Harvey Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," despite a movement among some employees and investors to bring him back.

Why it matters: Nvidia remains squarely at the center of the AI economy — and investors are anxious for guidance on the company's strategic roadmap following Huang's sudden exit.

Zoom in: Nvidia recorded quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion and net income of $65.3 billion, both more than doubling its performance from a year earlier.

- S&P Capital IQ analysts had expected revenue of $108.7 billion and net income of $60.5 billion.

- The company's data center revenue — its largest segment — rose 110% from a year ago to $107.6 billion.

The showing — which included a whopping gross profit margin of 74.8% — immediately calmed investors who have shaved more than $1 trillion off of Nvidia's market cap since the Huang scandal broke.

- The stock was up as much as 5% in after-hours trading Tuesday following the company's earnings call.

It marks the first moment for interim CEO Colette Kress — who is also still serving as CFO — to exert her leadership during the most tumultuous moment in the company's history.

- On the earnings call, she struck a steady tone, saying the company's momentum and innovation remain substantial.
- "We've got this," she said.
- Kress didn't mention Huang by name on the call but acknowledged the turbulent time and the "diverse" opinions — an apparent reference to the campaign to bring back Huang.

What we're watching: WSJ reported Tuesday afternoon that the SEC has opened a civil probe into the company.

- An SEC spokesperson declined to comment. 
- Nvidia reps did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and the executives didn't discuss it on the call — but they said their quarterly 10Q filing with the SEC will be delayed.

The earnings call came hours after Huang posted a public letter saying he'd be "honored" to return as CEO.

- Tech analyst Dan Ives earlier said the board could face a special shareholder meeting called by at least 15% of shareholders.
- Jones said the company would not take questions on Huang's exit. He said the board would consider external and internal candidates and wants to move "as quickly as possible" but won't make a move until it finds "exactly the right person."
