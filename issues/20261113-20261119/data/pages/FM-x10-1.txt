# FINRA Orders Spartan Capital Securities to Pay $3.18 Million for Fraud and Private Placement Supervisory Failures

### Firm Ordered to Pay $2.18 Million in Restitution and $1 Million Fine; Former CEO Suspended and Former Acting CCO Barred

WASHINGTON—FINRA has ordered Spartan Capital Securities, LLC to pay $2.18 million in restitution to customers and a $1 million fine for misconduct involving recommendations of private placements in pre-initial public offering (IPO) companies issued by the firm’s affiliates, known as the Atlas Funds. 

The misconduct included failures to conduct reasonable due diligence on the offerings and fully disclose conflicts of interest, as well as the use of offering documents that misrepresented material facts, in violation of Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) and the anti-fraud provisions of the Securities Act of 1933. 

In a settlement, which resolved a pending disciplinary complaint, Spartan agreed to pay the restitution and fine. The settlement also included a two-year suspension in all capacities of John Lowry, Spartan's majority owner, former CEO and the sole owner of the Atlas Funds, as well as a $25,000 fine for his role in the misconduct. The firm's former acting chief compliance officer and former chief administrative officer, Kim Monchik, was barred from associating with any FINRA member firm. 

Pre-IPO private placements can offer exposure to private companies that are anticipated to go public through a stock exchange listing. Such investments may involve funds that pool investor money to acquire shares or future interests in those companies, so investors hold interests in the funds rather than owning shares in the private companies directly. In this case, Spartan’s customers purchased interests in the Atlas Funds, which invested in other pre-IPO funds called SP Venture Funds, managed by StraightPath Venture Partners LLC. 

"Investors rely on their brokerage firms to reasonably evaluate the investments they recommend and to provide accurate information about investment costs and financial conflicts of interest," said Bill St. Louis, Executive Vice President and Head of Enforcement at FINRA. "Spartan failed to conduct reasonable due diligence and, together with its former executives, provided misleading information about markups that benefited entities controlled by its CEO. This action orders restitution to affected customers and holds the firm and its former executives accountable."

Findings

Between March and October 2021, Spartan recommended 344 investments in the Atlas Funds, totaling approximately $24 million, to 186 customers, most of whom were retail investors. FINRA found that the firm did not reasonably investigate the offerings before making recommendations and did not fully and fairly disclose its conflicts of interest.
