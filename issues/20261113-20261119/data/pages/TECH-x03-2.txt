# Nvidia reassures employees as it seeks new CEO after Huang's exit

Nvidia pledges continuity as interim CEO rules out Huang's return

입력 2026.11.19. 07:35업데이트 2026.11.19. 07:58

Nvidia, the leading artificial intelligence (AI) Semiconductor stock, is rallying its employees as it seeks to recover from the shock of founder Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO. Interim CEO Colette Kress on the 18th ruled out a return by Huang, who left the company amid a scandal, and made clear that Nvidia intends to maintain its AI Semiconductor leadership despite the change in management.

Kress held a town hall meeting for Nvidia's more than 47,300 employees that day at its headquarters in Santa Clara, California, taking the stage alongside board members and about 30 senior executives. "Nvidia's ambitions will not change," she said. "Neither will our passion for innovation or our commitment to customers, partners and shareholders." Kress was chief financial officer (CFO) before taking over as Nvidia's interim leader after Huang stepped down.

Nvidia headquarters in Santa Clara, California, on the 12th. /Courtesy of Yonhap News Agency

Nvidia has been thrown into turmoil since Huang resigned after leading the company for more than 30 years. Its market capitalization has shrunk to $3.66 trillion (about 4,880 trillion won) from nearly $6 trillion (about 8,000 trillion won) a month ago. Amid the turmoil, an outside campaign to bring Huang back is underway, while the board has dismissed the idea. Board chair Harvey Jones said at the event that it would be "unrealistic to think that we can find a Jensen clone," but added that "there are dynamic leaders out there with proven intellect, advanced engineering experience and strategic thinking," signaling the board's intent to recruit a new leader.

Executives stressed that Nvidia's business strategy remains unchanged despite Huang's departure. Ian Buck, head of the data center division, expressed confidence in the company's roadmap for developing next-generation AI chips, saying, "We know how to get it done." Nvidia plans to complete its $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, an open AI model platform, as planned and maintain its partnership with TSMC, the world's largest Foundry (contract semiconductor manufacturing) company.

Employees remain divided over Huang's departure. AI engineer Nick Spirin expressed regret on social media, calling Huang "the soul and brain of the company and the ecosystem." Other employees welcome the change, saying Huang grew close to President Donald Trump over the past year and undermined industry plans to assess AI safety, The New York Times (NYT) reported.
