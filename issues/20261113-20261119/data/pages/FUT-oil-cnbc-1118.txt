# Oil prices tumble as traders weigh surplus supply outlook 

Published Tue, Nov 17 2026 10:01 PM EST Updated Wed, Nov 18 2026 5:12 PM EST

Oil prices fell on Wednesday on data showing an increase in U.S. crude and fuel inventories last week, although ongoing U.S.-Iran attacks and disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz limited losses.

Brandon Bell | Getty Images News | Getty Images

Oil prices fell on Wednesday after US crude and fuel inventories posted larger-than-expected rises, according to the Energy Information Administration, adding to expectations of a supply surplus.

Brent crude futures lost 1.28% to settle at $91.95 a barrel, while West Texas Intermediate futures settled down 3.2% at $83.26.

U.S. crude inventories rose by 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million barrels in the week ended November 13, the EIA said, compared with analysts' expectations in a Reuters poll for a rise of 58,000 barrels.

U.S. gasoline stocks rose by ⁠5.2 million ‌barrels in the week to 215.9 million barrels, the EIA said, compared with analysts' expectations ⁠in a Reuters poll for a build of 1.6 million barrels.

Distillate stockpiles, which include diesel and heating oil, rose by 1.6 million barrels in the week to 104.9 million barrels, versus expectations for a rise of 1.3 million barrels, the EIA data showed.

Those stock increases renewed selling pressure, Gelber & Associates analysts said, adding: "Attacks involving Iran and commercial shipping still threaten deliveries through the ‌Strait of Hormuz, but those risks have provided limited support against the growing expectation of a global surplus."

Meanwhile, both Brent and U.S. crude futures contracts were on track for declines of about 20% this month on rising exports from the Middle East, as signaled ​by plummeting shipping rates.

The Brent/WTI spread was trading at its widest in about two weeks on Tuesday, LSEG data showed, with WTI logging steeper declines during trading on Wednesday.

"WTI arbitrage trades have been shut to Asia and Northwest Europe, thus WTI correcting more than Brent is the expected price movement to allow the arbitrage window to open again," said June Goh at Sparta Commodities, adding that rising U.S. Gulf Coast ⁠crude stocks are further contributing to the widening spread.

### U.S.-Iran conflict 

But the continuing U.S.-Iran conflict and the risks to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz curbed price declines.

Iran said ‌it had attacked U.S. drone and missile sites in Kuwait overnight after the U.S. struck Qeshm ‌Island in the Strait of Hormuz. It was Tehran's first attack on the U.S.-allied Gulf state since September. Bahrain, home of the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet, said it intercepted Iranian attacks.

Both oil benchmarks had settled higher on Tuesday as attacks on ships in the Middle East intensified, even as talks continued between Iran and mediators from Oman and Qatar.
