# Oil prices inch up as tanker disruptions offset surplus supply outlook

Published Wed, Nov 18 2026 10:19 PM EST Updated 4 Hours Ago

Oil prices were steady on Thursday after falling previously as increasing expectations for a supply surplus and higher interest rates softened sentiment, outweighing support from attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz amid the lingering Iran war.

Brandon Bell | Getty Images

Oil ​prices inched higher on Thursday as investors focused ​on fresh reports of tanker disruptions in the ​Strait of Hormuz, helping the market recover from the previous session's selloff driven by rising US inventories and surplus forecasts.

Brent crude futures Add To Watchlist rose 77 cents, or 0.8%, to $92.72 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures Add To Watchlist for December delivery gained $1.08, or 1.3%, to $84.34. The December contract expires on Friday, while ⁠the ‌more actively traded January contract Add To Watchlist advanced 91 cents, or 1.1%, to $83.21, with trading volumes ⁠nearly seven times those of the front-month contract.

Two oil tankers hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday and "exploded," Iran's navy said in a statement carried by state media. Reuters could not independently verify the report.

The Iran-aligned Houthi movement also fired missiles at the Saudi Arabian capital Riyadh.

Earlier in the day, two Western officials briefed by the Saudis said the kingdom is preparing for ​war against Yemen's Houthis to stretch well into next year.

"Rising Iranian attacks are weighing on Middle Eastern exports just as China ramps up crude imports, reducing oil on water and tightening the oil market," said UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo.
